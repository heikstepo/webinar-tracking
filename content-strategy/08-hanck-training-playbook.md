### On the niche choice

JJ's USP and Rauf's are structurally the same kind of thing — a differentiated read on the
market. The difference is **what sits in the title**. Hanck picked prop firms as the niche
because it is aspirational and searched; the USP then *filters* that broad niche.

### On ICP — the most transferable idea in the training

> *"If you try and attract more intermediate to advanced traders and older people, you will

> sell to because first of all, they probably don't have enough pain. They haven't gone

He ties this directly to the sales metrics: *"if you want to have the highest pif rate...

And the diagnostic loop back from those metrics to content:

> *"Is the AOV down? AOV might be down because the trading content isn't attracting older

### Zero lifestyle content

## 3. TOF / MOF / BOF — in his words

> *"Top of funnel typically tends to be broader videos that gets more views, that gets more
> leads, but **doesn't sell that good**. Then middle of funnel, which tends to be more niched
> down, a little bit more advanced, that **nurtures** people. And bottom of funnel content is
> to **sell** people. It's way more niche, way more advanced, and it's just there to convert."* — [0:16:03]

> *"You need a mixture of all of them. Imagine if you were only posting top of funnel — you
> won't get that many conversions. If you're only posting bottom of funnel, you won't get
> many leads."* — [0:16:48]

**He does not give the percentages in the video** — *"the percentages between each top of
funnel, middle of funnel and bottom of funnel is something we go over inside our program"*
[0:19:03]. Anyone quoting a specific split from this training is inventing it.

### What each stage looks like

| Stage | His description | Examples he names |
|---|---|---|
| **TOF** | Broad. *"This could be any candle ever — these are just broad videos to get leads"* [0:17:33] | *"The EXACT Trading Strategy That Made Me $1,200,000"* (75K) · *"I made $1.5M trading and learned this"* (~100K) · *"This ONE Candle Makes me $100,000+ PER MONTH"* |
| **MOF** | Niched, advanced, nurtures. **Includes the documentary series** — *"these are middle of funnel pieces because it's not super broad, but it's targeted towards intermediate traders"* [0:40:22] | The Road-to-$X weekly episodes |
| **BOF** | *"Back testing, some spreadsheet stuff, super niched down — beginners wouldn't even understand it. And it could also be **testimonials on YouTube**"* [0:22:04] | Backtests, student testimonials |

> **Correction to my earlier analysis.** In `07-content-only-breakdown.md` I classified the

> His BOF is narrower than mine: backtests, spreadsheet-level analysis, and testimonials.
> The stage splits I published in `07` (≈41/27/32) use *my* definitions, not his — see §9.

### The buying journey

> *"The leads that you get through these top of funnel videos typically binge watches your
> middle of funnel videos, gets warmed up, then goes through your bottom of funnel and
> converts, book a call."* — [0:18:18]

`cold → TOF → warm → MOF → hot → BOF → books a call`

## 4. The CTA rules — one per stage

**This is the direct answer to "how and when do they CTA."**

| Stage | CTA | His words |
|---|---|---|
| **TOF** | **Freebie or free community** — never the offer | *"More broader videos should do a CTA to a freebie or a free community"* [0:21:18] |
| **MOF** | **Soft mentorship mention** | *"Soft mentorship mentions works pretty good in middle of funnel content"* [0:22:04] |
| **BOF** | **Direct pitch** | *"Direct pitches works pretty good in bottom of funnel content... that's where the conversion happens"* [0:22:04] |

The reason, stated plainly:

> *"It would be weird posting a top of funnel video and then hard pitching to a mentorship
> when typically the views that come through top of funnel videos, they haven't seen your
> face before. It would just be weird getting thrown a CTA straight in their face when it's
> the first video they've watched. But if you throw it at their face after they watched two
> top of funnel videos, two middle of funnel videos and then one bottom of funnel video,
> then they'll convert."* — [0:22:49]

### The freebie mechanism

> *"It could be a free PDF or free journal or free whatever. In order for your viewers to get
> access to that, they need to opt in through an opt-in form to give their email. And then
> you send them email sequences over time to nurture them — cold to warm to hot."* — [0:21:18]

**Rauf has no freebie and no opt-in.** His TOF videos point at a Discord — which is the
right *category* of TOF CTA in this framework, but it captures no email, so there is no
nurture sequence behind it.

> JJ's description ladder, which I pulled separately, matches this exactly: the offer, then
> `go.jjsimontrades.com/freesite` (the freebie), then a student testimonial video, then the
> free Discord.

## 5. The golden rule of every CTA: sell the next click

> *"The golden rule behind copywriting is you should **never sell the program**. When we
> create a VSL we don't try and sell the program — we sell the next click. Same with story
> sequences. You should always sell the next step, because that next step takes you to the
> next step, and then all of a sudden they're on a booked call instead of you just trying to
> get someone to buy. **That's why we don't have checkout links either** — it doesn't make
> sense. You should sell the booking first, then the application, then the post-booking, and
> then you sell on the call."* — [1:50:17]

The two levers underneath it:

> *"There's **pain** — someone stops scrolling because you name their exact pain and their
> exact situation. And there's **curiosity** — you get them to click by hinting at a solution
> without fully handing it over."* — [1:50:17]

**What this means for a video CTA:** the job is never "buy my mentorship." It is *"click the
link."* One step only.

## 6. Hook → Retention → Reward

> *"Hook, retention, reward. That's how everything works. Long form, short form, story
> sequences, emails — in everything marketing-wise you need to get people hooked, keep them
> retained, and then give them some kind of reward at the end."* — [0:25:52]

| Part | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | *"The first five to ten seconds."* Two layers — the **visual** hook (what you show) and the **verbal** hook (what you say) [0:19:48] |
| **Retention** | *"Switching up in between — chart breakdowns, live annotations, trade recaps, live trading"* [0:19:48] |
| **Reward** | The stage-appropriate CTA from §4 |

The algorithmic rationale he gives:

> *"YouTube rewards a video that gets clicked on and that gets watched. So click-through rate
> must be high and average duration must be high."* — [0:20:33]

Which makes **thumbnail and title** the CTR lever and **retention edits** the duration lever.

## 7. The documentary series — the single biggest missing asset

He told JJ to build this **on the first call**:

> *"You should do a series where you document your journey to another $1 million in payouts."* — [0:24:20]

| Platform | Cadence | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram (short form)** | **Daily** | "Day 1, Day 2, Day 3…" |
| **YouTube (long form)** | **Weekly** | "Episode 1, 2, 3, 4…" |

Content: *"showing the trades, wins, **losses**, payouts."* [0:40:22]

### Why it works — session time

> *"There's a metric called session time. Average view duration is per video. Session time is
> how many videos they watch on average before clicking off your profile. When you create
> content that is binge-watch worthy — a documentary where you go over day one, day two,
> week one, week two — they can always go back and watch day one and then watch the entire
> documentary. So there's so much session time being added, which will boost your entire
> profile."* — [0:39:36]

### And FOMO

> *"A viewer might see your first video at day number 67. He checks out your profile and
> scrolls all the way down to day number one, because he wants to see how you got to
> $500,000. And what does that viewer do? He binges the entire documentary."* — [0:38:49]

> *"If that viewer watches day two, three, four, five, all the way up to day 30, and all of a
> sudden JJ has made $100,000 extra in payouts — that viewer gets insane FOMO."* — [0:41:08]

### The asset argument

> *"Reels shouldn't be taken that seriously. You shouldn't spend all your time on short form
> because short form will get forgotten after a week. But story sequences and YouTube videos —
> they can binge watch. It's an asset that'll make you money forever."* — [0:30:26]

**Rauf has no series of any kind** beyond "Module 1–4," which is a curriculum, not a
documented journey, and averages 2,800–4,500 views.

## 8. Instagram and YouTube as a flywheel

> *"On Instagram, you build **trust** through daily proof — your face gets shown to your
> followers every single day. YouTube builds **authority** and YouTube **sells** people."* — [0:34:16]

> *"It's engineered to constantly feed each other."* — [0:35:02]

> *"You don't need Twitter. You don't need TikTok."* — [0:35:02]

### The three pinned Instagram reels

Every one of these is prescribed:

1. **Strategy breakdown with numbers** — and the CTA is **to YouTube**, not to DMs:
   > *"Not DMs, not hard CTA, not hard pitching — just YouTube. You want all the people from
   > Instagram converted over to YouTube."* [0:43:24]
2. **Day 1–3 of the documentary** — so a viewer who lands on day 70 can find the start
3. **Pure proof** — payouts, tax reports, business banks

> *"All of these three reels is enough for a fresh lead that's skeptical about trading gurus
> to understand that JJ is actually legit."* [0:44:09]

### Story sequences

Same hook → retention → CTA structure, saved to **highlights** so they become permanent
assets. He claims three sequences produced **$40k / $40k / $40k on back-to-back days**
[0:27:24].

**Rauf's Instagram is currently a link in his channel description and nothing more in this
system.**

## 9. Track revenue per video, not views

The clearest statement in the training, and it is the same conclusion the earlier analysis
reached independently:

> *"Let's say a trading video of yours gets 100K views, but you then find out it makes $0
> revenue. That's not optimal — compared to another video getting 5K or 10K views but it
> brings in six figures in revenue. Then you need to know that this brings in revenue, so
> you can double down on **that** video."* — [1:26:46]

> *"We even got a video that made **$900,000 from one YouTube video**. Last 30 days we cash
> collected and tracked over **$1.4 million** for JJ through organic trading content."* — [1:26:46]

He uses a dedicated organic-attribution tool, plus a **post-purchase market research form**:

> *"Once someone joins your program you need to understand why they joined, what made them
> join, what was the tipping point, where did they find your content — was it Instagram, was
> it YouTube, **which specific YouTube video**, how long was the buying journey, what almost
> stopped them from buying. If you see that everyone joined from one YouTube video, you
> double down on that concept and make a new one."* — [1:14:35]

**This is buildable today in Rauf's existing stack** — the Typeform and Airtable tables are
already there (`06-metrics.md`).

> **On the stage splits in `07-content-only-breakdown.md`:** those percentages come from a
> keyword classifier using broader stage definitions than Hanck's. Re-running it under his
> narrower BOF definition collapses everyone's BOF share — JJ to 5%, Hanck's own channel to
> 4% — which mostly demonstrates that **stage cannot be read reliably from titles alone**
> under his framework, since it depends on what is actually inside the video. Treat the `07`
> splits as directional, and this document as the authoritative statement of the method.
