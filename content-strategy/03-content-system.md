# The Content System

Everything here is derived from what measurably works on **Rauf's own channel**,
structured the way JJ Simon structures his. Where the two conflict, Rauf's data wins —
his audience has already voted.

---

## 1. The proof number

**This is the first content decision, and it gates everything else.**

JJ has one number that *is* his brand — "$1,500,000+ In Verifiable Prop Firm Payouts" —
and it escalates in public across his uploads: $1.2M → $1.3M → $1.5M → $1.6M → $1.8M.

Rauf's numbers are scattered across 59 videos and never add up to anything:
$190,000 · $67,000 · $56,700 · $42,000 · $40,000 · $30,000 · $15,000 · $13,470.

### The action

1. Add up **every documentable payout** Rauf has receipts for.
2. That total becomes **the number** — it goes in the channel description, the banner,
   the landing page headline, and the pinned comment.
3. **It escalates on camera.** Every payout recap moves it up. The number climbing is
   the story.
4. It is stated the same way every time. Consistency is what makes it stick.

> If the honest total is $190,000, use $190,000. A real, documented, escalating
> $190,000 outperforms a borrowed $1.5M — because the receipts are the asset, and one
> unverifiable claim destroys the credibility of every true one next to it.

Channel description rewrite:

> **$XXX,XXX+ in verified prop firm payouts. I'm the first to break down time based
> ranges using real statistical data — one 30-minute window a day.**

That merges his real differentiator with JJ's proof-first structure.

## 2. The four formats

Eight videos a month, published **Tuesday and Saturday**.

| # | Format | Per month | Job | Judge it by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mechanism Reveal** | 3 | Reach + prove competence | Views, watch time |
| 2 | **Payout Recap (serialized)** | 2 | Trust + ritual | Returning viewers, applications |
| 3 | **Belief-Shift Essay** | 2 | Reframe so TBR is the only answer | Views, comments |
| 4 | **Student Proof** | 1 | Kill "works for you, not me" | **Close rate on calls** |

### 2.1 Mechanism Reveal — the workhorse (3/month)

His proven format. Every one of his top videos is this. Teach one specific,
named, time-based setup, completely, for free.

**What his data says the title must do:** name the system or the session, promise
*less* work, attach one concrete number.

Winners: *"This Asia Session Strategy is **Boring** but It Makes Money"* (45K) ·
*"**Stupid Simple** London Session Strategy (+$42,000)"* (18K) ·
*"I've Been Hiding The **Full Time Based Ranges System — Here It Is (A-Z)**"* (22K)

**Give away the whole mechanism.** The offer is not information — it is feedback,
accountability, and a room. Withholding the mechanism kills reach and buys nothing.

### 2.2 Payout Recap — the missing engine (2/month)

**Rauf has zero of these. It is his single biggest structural gap.**

A weekly serialized show: the trades, the payouts, the running total.
*"Road to $500,000 — Ep. 4"*

Expect these to **underperform on views**. JJ's serialized videos average 14,333
against a 19,000 channel median. That is not failure — it is the format doing a
different job. It converts casual viewers into returning viewers who watch a number
climb, and it is what makes a $4,500 purchase feel safe.

**Include the losses.** JJ published *"I Lost $30,000 in Prop Firm Profits in One
Day."* The losses are what make the wins believable, and they pre-handle the biggest
objection on the sales call.

### 2.3 Belief-Shift Essay (2/month)

Argue *the frame*, not the tactic — so that Rauf's system becomes the obvious
conclusion rather than one option among many. JJ's best-performing non-strategy video
is exactly this: *"If You Don't Understand Prop Firms, You Don't Understand Trading"* (44K).

Rauf's angle is stronger than JJ's here and he barely uses it: **he has statistics.**
"Chart patterns are subjective, time is measurable, here is the data" is a real
argument, not a slogan. It sets up the offer perfectly.

### 2.4 Student Proof (1/month)

He has **one** student video (1K views). This format has the largest direct effect on
close rate, because it is the answer to the last objection every prospect raises.

Formats: a 60-day results interview, a screen-share of a student's journal and payout,
or JJ's move — fly students out and film it (*"I Flew 4 Students to Miami"*).

## 3. Title formula

```
[Named system or session]  +  [Simplicity or scale claim]  +  [One specific number]
```

### Rules from his own data

**Do:**
- Name it — *Time Based Ranges*, *London*, *New York*, *Asia*, *Apex*, *Lucid*.
  Top-15 videos are **4x more likely** to brand the named system than bottom-15.
- Promise less work — *Boring · Stupid Simple · All You Need · 30 Minutes · One Candle · A-Z*
- Attach one number, and make it the cumulative one where honest
- Keep it under ~70 characters so it survives mobile truncation

**Don't:**
- ❌ Vague drama — *"The Data Will Shock You"* (2.3K), *"Will Change Your 2026"* (1.4K)
- ❌ Negate instead of name — *"I Stopped Using Chart Patterns"* (1.6K)
- ❌ Say "SECRET" without specifics — *"SECRET News Trading Strategy"* (2.1K)
- ❌ Curriculum labels — *"Module 1/2/3/4"* (2.8K–4.5K). Nobody clicks a syllabus.

> **The dollar sign is not the lever.** `$` appears in 4 of his top 15 titles and 4 of
> his bottom 15 — it does nothing on its own. What separates them is a **named system**
> plus a **simplicity promise**. Add the number to a title that already does those two
> things; it will not rescue one that doesn't.

|---|---|

## 5. Video structure and CTA placement

For a 10–14 minute Mechanism Reveal:

| Timestamp | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Hook** | The claim + the number. No intro, no logo, no "what's up guys" |
| 0:15–0:45 | **Proof** | Show the receipt on screen — payout, statement, backtest |
| 0:45–1:00 | **Promise** | Exactly what they'll be able to do by the end |
| 1:00–2:00 | **Context** | Why the standard approach fails; why time solves it |
| 2:00 | **Soft CTA** | *"Full system and my payout receipts are in the free training — link in the description."* One line, keep moving |
| 2:00–9:00 | **Deliver** | Teach it completely. Live chart. Real examples. No withholding |
| 9:00–10:30 | **Proof stack** | 2–3 more examples, including one that failed |
| 10:30–11:30 | **Hard CTA** | See below |
| 11:30–12:00 | **End screen** | Booking page + next video |

### The hard CTA — qualify, don't beg

> *"If you're trading a funded account or close to passing an evaluation, and you want
> me to build this system around your schedule and your firm — there's an application
> linked below. It's not for beginners, and I take a limited number of students each
> month. If that's you, fill it out and we'll get on a call."*

Four things it does: **qualifies** (funded or close), **sets scarcity** (limited),
**disqualifies** (not for beginners — which raises the value), **states the next step**.

## 6. Cadence and the 30-day recalibration

| | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Videos/month | ~5 | **8** (Tue + Sat) |
| Shorts/week | 0 | 3 — clip the long-forms, same CTA |
| Community posts/week | — | 2 — payout screenshots, student wins |

**At day 30, stop guessing.** The `utm_content` = video ID setup in
`02-offer-and-funnel.md` means the dashboard can rank every video by applications,
calls, and closed revenue. From month two forward:

> Make more of what produced **calls**, not what produced **views**.

These are frequently not the same videos. A 45K-view mechanism reveal that produces two
applications is worth less than a 6K-view student-proof video that produces six.
The dashboard in this repo already computes `closeRate`, `showRate`, `cashPerCall`,
and `avgOrderValue` — segment them by `utm_content` and let that pick the calendar.
