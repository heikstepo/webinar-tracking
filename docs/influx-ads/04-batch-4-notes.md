### Risk: "All engagement to your profile is organic and authentic" (slide 8)
- On the HeyGen call, the Media Engine was described as owned theme pages and creator profiles engaging with posts. In the Demandbase call, it was pages with "authority scores" that "give the algorithm the signals."
- A blanket "all engagement is organic" claim is the one line a skeptical buyer, LinkedIn or a Meta reviewer could challenge.
- **For ads:** stay with "targeted distribution creates early relevance signals" and "warm leads from real ICP buyers." Never claim "100% organic."

### V3 is still exec-framed
- "B2B Executives," "Executive Expertise," "launch executive(s)."
- The VSL and brief rescale this to founders of 10-20 person companies. For Meta, use the founder framing. V3 is the language source, not the identity callout.
- V3 has **no 30-in-30 guarantee and no price.** The guarantee lives only in the VSL, brief and calls.

## 2. The two new case studies: NOT ad-safe as they stand

### A. "Activating Authority for a Government Technology Leader"
- **Client:** about 2,000 employees, serving 7,000+ government organisations in the US, UK and Australia.
- **Execs:** SVP of Federal Sales + CTO.
- **"Unified Authority Flywheel":** 5 posts a week for each exec plus the company page (15 total), including **short-form video.**
- **6-month results:** $4.2M inbound pipeline; sales cycle cut from 300-365 to 240 days; 11 speaking invitations; 3 enterprise AEs and 5 senior engineers hired, all citing leadership visibility.

### B. "Pivoting a Federal IT Firm to a New Market"
- **Client:** about 150 employees, 25+ years, DoD and Intelligence Community work. The exec is the founder and CEO.
- **"Authority Flywheel,"** 12-month engagement.
- **Results:** $7.8M qualified pipeline in the new market; $2.5M prime contract; 3 cloud-provider partnerships; **40% valuation increase** "as confirmed by their external advisors."

### Why I'm holding both back from ads (and arguably from sales)
1. **They don't reconcile with the documented GovTech case.** That one is CEO + CRO, started Dec 2025 / Jan 2026, and is measured in leads and impressions. Case A is SVP Federal Sales + CTO, measured in pipeline dollars.
2. **The method doesn't match how Influx describes itself.** Short-form video (Alex told Mario "it's not video interviews, we protect your time"), 15 posts a week, and "Flywheel" branding instead of Authority Engine / Media Engine.
3. **Timeline.** Alex told Gary that Influx has been an agency "officially one year." A completed 12-month engagement and a 6-month one are possible only if they predate the agency (Alex consulting since 2019). Needs confirmation.
4. **They are made of claim types the VSL and brief ban:** pipeline dollars, contract wins, valuation uplift, attributed revenue. There is no lead or impression data, which is the core metric of the current offer.
5. **ICP mismatch even if real.** 150-2,000 employee federal and GovTech firms, not $1-10M founder-led companies.

**Ruling needed:** are these real, documented clients with permission to reference them? Or are they illustrative or template case studies? If illustrative, they should never touch ads. Paid social claims must be substantiated, and Meta and FTC rules on testimonials and results apply.

### What's still usable from them (qualitatively, no numbers)
- **Language:** "deep industry knowledge was trapped in emails and private conversations"; "their brand was synonymous with past performance, not future innovation"; "the company page was a simple repository for press releases."
- **Secondary benefits of visibility** (speaking invitations, better hires, partner intros) match what Rachel at Demandbase said she wants ("double take," "speaking invites," "reporters," "at bats"). Use only as "what visibility tends to unlock," never as a claimed result, unless one is documented.
