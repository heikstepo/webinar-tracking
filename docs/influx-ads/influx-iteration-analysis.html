The short version

If I only keep ten things from this, it's these.

  1. One chain, nothing else.What they do wrong, why that kills it, the fix. Every extra idea got cut, by you or by me after you complained.
  2. Build the chain like a staircase."If nobody cares… nobody reacts. And if nobody reacts… LinkedIn won't push it." Each line picks up the last line's ending.
  3. The fix mirrors the problem.Each step of the fix answers one link in the chain. "2 things" because there were 2 broken links.
  4. Tie the fix to their goal straight away."So if you want LinkedIn to push your posts so you can get qualified calls coming in…"
  5. No company name.The fix gets explained on its own. "We" comes in late, at the proof or the CTA.
  6. Name only the unique piece."I call this the media engine…" right where it's explained, then "Because…" to say what it does.
  7. Every reason needs a source.Alex, a call, a case study or Semrush. "First hour" was mine and it got caught.
  8. Talk, don't write.No analogies, no dramatic lines, no clever endings. If it sounds like a voiceover, it's wrong.
  9. Every ad opens differently.Different hook type, different reason, different proof. Never the same opener twice in a group.
  10. Give the copy, not an essay.When you ask for the ad, you get the ad. Explanations only when you ask "what are you trying to say".

Flow

The shape your 1.2 settled into. For problem ads, this is the shape until you say otherwise.

  1. Sweeping claim"Most founders just never grow on LinkedIn simply because…" Confident, about them, no setup.
  2. Their world, read outTwo or three things they actually post or say, in quotes, on screen.
  3. The staircase"The problem with that is… And if… And if… That simple… that's just how it works."
  4. Fix tied to their goal"So if you want X so you can get qualified calls coming in… you need 2 things."
  5. First / secondEach step answers one link of the staircase. A second line under each to make it concrete.
  6. Name the unique piece"I call this the media engine…" then "Because…" to say what it does.
  7. Proof, with "we""That's exactly what we did for Ryan…" What we did, then the before and after numbers.
  8. Qualifier"So if you're running a B2B company doing over a million a year… and [their problem]…"
  9. Two-layer CTA"…how we'd do both for you… [the front-end win]… AND 30 qualified calls booked in the next 90 days on the backend."
Seam testEvery line hands off to the next through a connector: "like…", "The problem with that is…", "And if…", "So if…", "First…", "And second…", "Because…", "That's exactly what…", "So if…". Read the last word of one line and the first word of the next, and the join should be obvious.

Style

What your lines do

  • One thought per line. Long ideas get split across two lines.
  • "…" at the end of a line when the thought carries on, and a full stop when it lands ("LinkedIn won't push it.").
  • Each line repeats the end of the last one: "nobody cares… / if nobody cares… nobody is going to react… / if nobody reacts…"
  • Digits for numbers ("2 things", "15 minutes", "1,059").
  • CAPS on one word in the whole ad ("AND").
  • Certain, no hedging: "the first 15 minutes", not "15 minutes or so".
  • Casual words: "stuff", "just", "simply", "that simple".

What gets cut every time

  • Analogies and metaphors.
  • Lines that sound like a voiceover ("a busy month killed it").
  • A second or third idea in the same ad.
  • The company name, and pivots like "that's what we do".
  • Claims I can't point to a source for.
  • The same hook opener, Influx line or proof as another creative in the group.
  • Long notes after the copy when you only asked for the copy.
