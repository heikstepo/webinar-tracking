Influx · Ideal client profile

Who the 30-in-30 offer is for, and who it isn't

This is a profile of the founder Meta ads should attract: their situation today, what they want, what's in the way, how aware they are, what they'll object to, and the exact words they use. It combines the brief's ICP definition with nine real sales and discovery calls.

Prepared 9 Oct 2026Evidence: brief, VSL, Deck V3, 9 callsCompanion: Offer Blueprint

How much to trust this

Only 2 of the 9 calls (Mario and Shikha, both on the new 30-in-30 pitch) sit inside the Meta ICP. The other 7 came from outbound and are larger companies. Their pains are real and repeat often, but they come from bigger organisations. Throughout this page, items backed by the two in-ICP calls are tagged, and anything inferred rather than observed is labelled inference.

The ICP

A founder who is both the face and the closer of a lean B2B company, whose best clients arrive through referrals, who knows LinkedIn should be bringing in pipeline, and who has no time, no system and no wish to become a content creator.

Role
Founder, CEO or senior exec who personally sells
Company
B2B, about $1–10M revenue. Form gate at ~$500k.
Team
About 10–20 people, little or no marketing staff
Sales motion
Founder-led. Referrals, network, outbound, events.
Deal size
Big enough that 1 or 2 clients cover the ~$14.7k trial inference
Market
US first (current Meta targeting). Some UAE interest.

The brief's direction of travel: "Influx is moving away from large enterprise and toward founder-led SMB and young mid-market buyers. Creative and targeting should reflect that." The Meta campaign targets the US, ages 25 to 64, with broad targeting. The ad itself has to call out who it is for.

Who we target, and who we don't

The "don't" list matters as much as the "do" list. Every off-ICP booking costs one of Alex's evening call slots.

Target

  • Founders, CEOs and senior execs who are the public face and the main closer
  • B2B companies at roughly $1–10M revenue. $1M and up is the safer band.
  • Lean teams of about 10–20 people, with no one owning the founder's LinkedIn
  • Pipeline that runs on referrals, network, events, outbound or RFPs
  • Offers valuable enough that one or two new clients pay for the trial
  • Buyers who are decision makers and check LinkedIn before they buy
  • Categories seen so far: professional services and consulting, IT and technology services, B2B software and AI, specialist advisory firms

Don't target

  • Companies under ~$500k revenue (the Typeform gate)
  • Enterprise as the default: multi-brand marketing departments, procurement, programs that need board sign-off
  • Teams that already write in-house and only want amplification. That's a different product.
  • Businesses whose buyers aren't on LinkedIn: corner shops, supermarket shoppers, solopreneurs who live on Facebook and Reddit
  • Buyers shopping for a cheap AI writer, follower counts or "an army to play the algorithm"
  • Anyone expecting booked meetings or closed deals within 30 days
  • Founders who won't define their ICP, approve content or follow up leads
  • Companies in a cash squeeze, waiting on a raise or "a few deals to close"
  • People whose real goal is video, podcasts or personal fame
Edge cases
  • $500k–1M revenue. Allowed through the form, but price bites hard. At ~$1.2M, Mario called a year of Influx "about 5% of our revenue... pretty much the full marketing budget for the year." Consider ads that call out "$1M+" or "$2–10M".
  • B2B2C. Mario's firm serves veterans, who are consumers. Alex pitched it anyway, since veterans can be found on LinkedIn. Judge case by case.

Fit test

A quick qualifier for the Typeform, the discovery call and the ad callouts.

Must have

  • B2B, or consumers clearly reachable on LinkedIn
  • The founder or exec is the face and the closer
  • Revenue of at least ~$500k, comfortably $1M+
  • Their buyers use LinkedIn
  • Will give 1 hour plus a monthly approval
  • Someone will follow up the weekly leads
  • Can pay ~$14.7k upfront without it being the whole marketing budget

Strong signals

  • Most new business comes from referrals or network
  • A growth target: double revenue, a new market, a new product
  • A real track record, but little visible online
  • Has tried posting, a ghostwriter or ads, and nothing stuck
  • Admires a peer who posts every week
  • Plans to hire salespeople soon

Red flags

  • Wants meetings guaranteed
  • Measures success in followers or impressions
  • Wants the cheapest writer possible
  • Won't approve content or define a buyer
  • Wants to write in-house and only boost
  • "I'm a bad customer right now", meaning cash is tight

Desired outcomes

Four layers. Ads lead with the commercial layer, and the personal layer is what gets them to book.

Commercial

  • More clients, contracts and revenue
  • Reach the next milestone faster: double or triple (Mario), "a million, two million" (Shikha)
  • Less risk if referrals slow down
"More contracts. More contracts. More contracts. That's basically it."Martyn, revenue lead, energy services

Pipeline

  • Warm inbound from ideal buyers, every week
  • Prospects who arrive already trusting them
  • Outbound that lands warm, because the market already knows them
"Recognized as the authority in our field and they want a slice. They want us to work on their projects."Martyn, revenue lead, energy services

Status

  • Be seen as the authority and "a player"
  • Speaking invites, podcast requests, peers asking how they do it
  • Become the founder they currently admire from a distance
"I'd want people doing a double take like, whoa, who's the CMO that's over here doing these things?"Rachel, CMO, enterprise martech

Personal

  • No writing and no filming
  • Content that sounds like them, never like AI or a hype merchant
  • Control over every word that goes out
"Even if I'm like 80% of where I want to be but still the right path, I would prefer that than going 120%."Shikha, founder, AI products and consulting

The pattern underneath

Referrals close because the trust already exists. Cold leads don't close because it doesn't. The ICP's best business comes from trust they didn't have to build, and they have no way to create that trust at scale. That gap is what the offer fills.

Retargeting

Influx has been an agency for about a year and has mostly sold through outbound, so very few cold viewers will know the name. Lead with the problem and the outcome, not the brand.

Market sophistication

How many similar promises this buyer has already heard. For "grow your LinkedIn" offers, the answer is a lot.

Stage 1First claim. "Grow your business with LinkedIn."
Stage 2Bigger claim. "10x your reach." "100k followers."
Stage 3Mechanism. "Our ghostwriting framework." "AI content engine."
Stage 4A better mechanism. Done for you, plus distribution, plus a scored lead list, plus a guarantee.
Stage 5Identification. They buy from whoever sounds like them: founder to founder, no hype.

Why stage 4 to 5

  • OJ gets "20, 40 a day" junk messages on LinkedIn, most of them pitches.
  • Gary: "The only things I get in my inbox right now are people trying to sell stuff."
  • Shikha had already compared vendors in ChatGPT, and had a $3k freelancer price anchor.
  • The VSL already pre-empts "agencies that sound like AI" and "vanity reach".

What that means for ads

  • Bigger numbers won't win. Everyone claims reach.
  • Win on mechanism: distribution to ideal buyers, plus a weekly list of who engaged.
  • Win on the scoreboard: leads, not likes, with no payment if it misses.
  • Win on identification: peer tone, anti-hype, for founders who'd rather not post.

Buying triggers

The moments that turn "I should" into "I'll take the call". These are good raw material for hooks.

  • Fear that referrals will dry up. Mario: "One day might be six months, might be three years."
  • A growth target referrals can't hit. Double or triple (Mario), "a million, two million" (Shikha), "more contracts" (Martyn).
  • A new market or segment. Mid-market chains (Mahdi), food service (Gary), Dubai (Shikha).
  • A launch with no sales motion behind it. Shikha: "What's the point of them being out there? I'm not selling them."
  • A fundraise or perception moment. OJ before a Series B.
  • A channel that stopped working. LinkedIn "gone off the boil" (Martyn), Meta leads not converting (Mario), outsourced sales development firms fired (OJ).
  • About to hire salespeople. Wants proof of demand first. Shikha's advisor told her to "go with the founder route of selling" before building a sales team.
  • Watching a peer win. Gary: "I admire the hell out of him."
  • Finding out what AI says about them. Shikha's ChatGPT moment.
  • A planning moment. A new fiscal quarter, Gary's "Chapter 4" strategy.

How they decide

Who and how

  • The founder decides. They pull in finance or accounting (Mario), the board (Gary), the CEO when the champion isn't the CEO (Martyn), or a new marketing hire (Rachel).
  • Two calls is normal: a discovery call, then a second call with a statement of work and an ROI projection.
  • Before they buy, they ask for an ROI mock-up for finance, content samples, case studies "in my space", and a trial with an out.
  • Shikha wanted a week to "mull it over" and was speaking to competitors.

What tips them, and what stalls them

  • The guarantee (Mario leaned in the moment he heard it)
  • Not having to film or write (Mario: "that's a lot of time")
  • The feel of the conversation. Shikha: "I like the vibe... Brits, they're known for no-nonsense approaches."
  • Alex's credentials (Shikha asked what being a Top Voice means)
  • Cash timing, and "not now"
  • Having to sell it internally
  • Shopping competitors, and a wrong price anchor from ChatGPT

Segments

Four candidate sub-groups inside the ICP. They're a starting point for ad groups, to be mapped onto your framework once we go through the training.

The Referral-Ceiling Founder

Evidence: Mario (strong), Martyn, OJ

Situation
Services or advisory firm. Most revenue comes from referrals and repeat clients, and growth is steady but feels too slow. Tried ads or events, and the leads didn't close.
Wants
A warm flow of buyers that doesn't depend on who happens to refer them.
Core idea
Make every new lead feel like a referral.

The Invisible Expert

Evidence: Gary, Shikha, Martyn's CEO, Mahdi

Situation
Deep expertise and a real track record, almost nothing online. Humble or just busy. Admires a peer who posts every week.
Wants
To be recognised as the authority without becoming an influencer.
Core idea
The market can't buy expertise it can't see.

The Founder-Seller

Evidence: Shikha, OJ

Situation
The product or offer is ready and the founder is the only closer. A sales team is next year's problem, and every dollar has to return something.
Wants
Pipeline now, built on the founder's own credibility.
Core idea
Before you hire a sales team, make the founder visible.

The Burned Buyer

Evidence: Mario, OJ, Rachel, Mahdi

Situation
Has already paid for a ghostwriter, an AI tool, an agency or cold lead gen, and got volume without trust.
Wants
Something judged on real buyers, with a way out if it doesn't work.
Core idea
Judged on leads. If it misses, you don't pay.
