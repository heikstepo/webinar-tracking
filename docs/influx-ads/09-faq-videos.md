# Influx pre-call FAQ videos (master)

15 Oct 2026. Short videos on the confirmation page, under the thank-you video. Alex to camera, split screen where something can be shown.

## How the PM Rev and Iron FAQ videos are built

1. The title is the question in the lead's own words, and the video opens by asking it again ("So… how does the PM Simulation Model actually work?", "Also… WIE genau bringt ihr uns auf 8-stellig?").
2. Acknowledge it straight: "Honestly… that's the first thing almost everyone asks me." / "Ehrliche Antwort: Das hängt davon ab…"
3. An insider reason or reframe (PM: "in 700+ interviews I got asked about my PMP 3 times"; Iron: there's no single lever, it's a compound effect).
4. The concrete answer, with the mechanism, numbers or a timeline, and something shown on screen (platform, roadmap, Slack, case screenshots).
5. A named proof case.
6. An honest caveat ("Not everyone gets there in 16 weeks", "eine neue Initiative knallt nicht immer sofort", "if things are tight, it's probably not the right time").
7. Close on the call, made specific: "On the call, we'll…".
Each is 100-250 words, one question per video (PM sometimes pairs two related ones).

## The 8 FAQs Influx actually needs

Ranked by how often they came up in the 9 sales and discovery calls plus Tony, and how much they block the booking or the close. Every answer reuses what the ads and the VSL already say, so it stays congruent.

1. **"How much of my time does this actually take? Do I have to write or film anything?"**
   - Why: "no time" came up in 7 of 9 calls and is the ICP's main reason LinkedIn never happens (OJ: "important but not as important as the 10 other things I have to do"; Mario: "it's not like making video interviews with me… because that's a lot of time"). Both PM and Iron have this one.
   - Answer: one onboarding call, about an hour; then about 10 minutes a month to look over posts; no writing, no filming; you or your sales team take the calls.
   - Open: the VSL says approval is "optional"; Alex's materials and ad 6.1 say nothing goes live without sign-off.

2. **"What does it cost, and is it worth it?"**
   - Why: cost came up with Mario, Shikha, OJ, Gary and Martyn's CEO. Mario called a year of it "pretty much the full marketing budget". Tony compared it to his marketing manager's salary. Both examples have a pricing FAQ.
   - Answer: Iron's shape. Not cheap, mid to high four figures a month depending on scope (one LinkedIn or the company page too), 3-month proof of value or 12 months; what's in it; set against what one client is worth and against hiring someone in-house; the guarantee; exact number on the call.
   - Open: Alex's OK on a range; [$X] average result.

3. **"Will the posts actually sound like me, not like AI?"**
   - Why: Mahdi, Shikha and Nicola raised AI-speak; Shikha also worried about "showing off every day". It's ad 6.1.
   - Answer: the onboarding hour captures their stories and opinions, our writers write in their voice with no AI-speak, nothing goes live without their OK, 95% approved first time. Authority content is useful thinking for their ICP, not self-promotion.

4. **"How does the media engine work, and is it safe for my account?"**
   - Why: it's the one claim nobody else makes, so sophisticated founders will wonder if it's bots, an engagement pod or paid ads (Rachel asked exactly that). Alex brings up account safety on every call.
   - Answer: why it exists (the algorithm pushes posts that get early reactions, and an audience normally takes months), what it is (20+ partner LinkedIn brands, 3M+ followers, reacting within 15 minutes), real LinkedIn members, no password, client-controlled encrypted access, within LinkedIn's terms, zero bans, 100M+ impressions. Also answers "I barely have any followers".
   - Open: Alex's framing warning ("in theory it isn't a practice LinkedIn supports"). He has to sign off on this script word for word.

5. **"Will these calls actually turn into clients?"**
   - Why: Mario, Shikha and Mahdi asked it ("do you think LQLs will possibly actually happen"). Mario's own cold leads closed at under 3%. Their best clients come from referrals, where the trust already exists.
   - Answer: these aren't cold leads. By the time they book, they've been reading your posts for weeks and already know, like and trust you (ad 4.1). They're qualified to your ICP. You or your sales team close, like a referral. Scott and Ryan: [X] booked calls, [$X] revenue.

6. **"How fast will I see booked calls, and how does the guarantee work?"**
   - Why: Tony asked if there's "any commitment to the numbers"; Nicola, Mahdi and Rachel asked how a lead is counted. The whole offer is 30 qualified calls in 90 days. PM pairs timeline and guarantee in one video.
   - Answer: realistic timeline (onboarding, first posts live in [X] days, first month launch and testing, first booked calls in [X] weeks, 30 by day 90), what counts as a qualified call, the guarantee (if we don't hit 30 in 90 days, we keep working at no extra cost until we do), what slows it down.
   - Open: qualified call definition, guarantee terms in the contract, [X] days and weeks. All Alex.

7. **"Does this work in my industry, for a company my size? Do you have results?"**
   - Why: Gary, OJ and Mario asked whether their clients are even on LinkedIn; Mahdi, Nicola, Gary and Shikha asked for examples in their space. It's ad 6.2. Both examples have a results FAQ.
   - Answer: the more niche the market, the easier it is to stand out; Ryan sells to government; Scott started from almost nothing; [CLIENT 3] and [CLIENT 4]; Iron's honest move of showing a below-average case and why.
   - Open: the dossier's gap, no case from a $1-10M company yet. More cases from Alex.

8. **"We've tried a ghostwriter, an agency, or someone on our team posts already. How is this different?"**
   - Why: the burned buyer segment (Mario, OJ, Rachel, Mahdi) and the already-posting group (Martyn, OJ, Nicola). Tony's marketing manager ran it and "the messaging wasn't great". It's ad 1.3.
   - Answer: they optimized for engagement, not calls. Writing was never the gap: strategy (TOF, MOF, BOF), distribution (media engine) and conversion (booking) are. If someone already posts, they can keep doing what works; the call works out whether they need the full system.

Left out on purpose:
- "Who's my contact, and is it group coaching?" (Iron): done for you, so it fits as one line inside FAQ 1.
- "Not now" timing: a booked lead has already decided to look; the soft close is the guarantee in FAQ 6.
- Contract length and payment options: inside FAQ 2.
