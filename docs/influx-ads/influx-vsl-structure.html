Influx · VSL prep · structure

How the two VSLs work

Both sell the call in the first two minutes. The rest of the video is for the people who aren't sold yet.

PM Revolution and Iron, broken into their beats with the lines that carry each one. Then what has to change for Influx (5-6 minutes, offer first, no price up front, a range is fine) and a proposed structure with a word budget for every section.

PM Rev: ~2,250 words, ~15 minIron: ~1,500 words, ~10 minInflux target: ~840 words, under 6 min15 Oct 2026

The short version

  1. The offer is the hook.Line 1 calls out who it's for, line 2 is the offer with a timeframe. No story, no question, no warm-up.
  2. The mechanism gets named, then held back."The way we'll do that is by using what I call the PM Simulation Model… And in the next few minutes I'll show you exactly how that works…" It's only explained halfway through.
  3. Proof before the pitch.By minute 1:40 you know who's talking, how big they are and three results from people like you.
  4. An early exit for buyers who are already sold."If you've heard enough… book a call right below this video." Everyone else is asked to stay a few more minutes.
  5. The middle explains why nothing has worked so far.Their situation, what they've tried, why it failed, then "THAT'S WHY what you ACTUALLY need is…", which is the mechanism.
  6. The offer comes back three times.At the open, after the mechanism and in the last line. Same words each time.
  7. They push people away to pull the right ones in.Who it isn't for, said bluntly. Iron: "Und falls ich dich damit gerade disqualifiziert hab… tut mir nicht leid."
  8. The CTA sells the call, not the program.What the form asks, what happens on the call, an honest no if it's not a fit, and something they get from the call either way.

Same skeleton, different lengths

Both run the same order. Iron is shorter because it folds objections and the case study into other sections. At 5-6 minutes, Influx has to do the same, and harder.

Beat by beat

Each beat with the line that carries it in PM Rev and in Iron, and what we take from it for Influx.

OfferWho it's for, the result, the timeframe

"If you've spent 5-10 years or more in another career… and you want to break into project management… I'll help you land your first project manager role within 16 weeks and support you through your first 90 days on the job…"

"Falls du eine Ecom Brand hast, die gerade bei 100 bis 500k Monatsumsatz ist… Wollen wir dir helfen, die Brand in den nächsten 3 Monaten auf 1 Mio im Monat zu skalieren mit unserem Ugly Funnel System…"

Same shape, with the ads' offer word for word: the callout, then "we'll build your LinkedIn brand and book 30 qualified calls in the next 90 days, completely done for you".

"Even if"Kill the first objections inside the offer

"Even if you have zero project management experience… No degree or certifications… And don't want to quit your current job to make the switch."

"Und dir parallel dein eigenes Team aufzubauen… damit das Ganze am Ende auch ohne uns läuft." (a second result instead)

We already have ours: "without you ever having to write a single post or step in front of a camera". One more at most, e.g. "even if you've barely posted before".

Open loopName the mechanism, promise the how

"The way we'll do that is by using what I call the PM Simulation Model… And in the next few minutes I'll show you exactly how that works, who I am, and how this whole consulting program is structured."

"Wie genau wir das machen, für wen das ist… und für wen halt auch nicht… zeig ich dir jetzt ganz stumpf in den nächsten paar Minuten hier… Aber keine Sorge, ich halt mich kurz…"

Name it in the first 30 seconds and promise "exactly how that works". Iron's "I'll keep it short" fits a 5-minute video.

ProofWho's talking, then people like you

"First off, my name's Nick and I've been a project manager earning high six figures for the past 18 years… And helped over 127 people break into project management… Like Justin… Sandra… Tom…"

"Also, kurz zu mir… über 500 Ecom Brands skaliert… IM8 von David Beckham… Aber eben auch Brands, die genau da angefangen haben, wo du jetzt stehst… NoMisk zum Beispiel von 100k auf 2,6 Millionen im Monat"

Alex first (his own LinkedIn), then Influx's total for clients, then Iron's turn, "founders who started right where you are", into Scott and Ryan, one line each.

Soft CTAEarly exit for the ones already sold

"Now, if you've heard enough… You can book a call with me right below this video… But… If you'd like to understand a bit more first… that's completely fine… Just stay with me for a few more minutes."

"Dann kannst du dir eigentlich jetzt schon direkt unter diesem Video einen Termin buchen… Weil wir uns die Kunden mittlerweile zum Glück aussuchen können… Jeder schlechte Kunde schadet mir nämlich mehr als dem Kunden selbst."

The ads already sold some of them, so let those book at about 1:30. If we use Iron's "we choose our clients", it has to be true for Influx.

BridgeInto their situation

"Because chances are… your situation looks a lot like most of the people I speak to every week…"

"Also, wenn du bei 100 bis 500k im Monat bist, sieht's bei dir wahrscheinlich genauso aus wie bei fast jeder Brand, die bei uns auf den Call kommt…"

"…your LinkedIn probably looks a lot like most of the founders I speak to every week."

Current stateWhat's fine, what isn't, what they tried

"You're good at what you do… on paper, everything's fine. But you probably feel capped, right?… So you look at your options… A certificate… Going back to school…"

"Product Market Fit ist da… die Ads bringen Umsatz… Aber sobald du das Budget hochdrehst… mehr Creatives, eine neue Agentur, noch ein Freelancer…"

The company's doing well, mostly from referrals, and you know LinkedIn works. You've posted when you found the time, handed it to someone on the team, maybe tried a ghostwriter or AI. That's AG1 in one breath.

ReframeThe real reason, with insider proof

"All those options… they actually don't matter… See, in all the 500+ interviews I've been through… I got asked about my PMP 3 times…"

"Und genau das ist das Problem… Damit holen deine Ads nämlich fast nur eine Bewusstseinsstufe ab…"

The real reason: posts nobody outside the company cares about, nobody reacting early so LinkedIn never pushes them, and nothing that turns engagement into calls. Alex's insider proof goes here (how many founders' LinkedIn he's looked at).

CostWhat happens if nothing changes

"Imagine what'll actually happen day 1… a lot of people in that spot end up getting fired within their first few months."

"Solange sich daran nichts ändert, bleibst du genau da, wo du jetzt bist… egal, wie viel Geld du noch reinsteckst."

One line, in Iron's tone: you stay where you are, on referrals you can't control. No guilt bait.

Need → mechanismThe turn into the open loop

"THAT'S WHY… what you ACTUALLY need is to get that experience before anyone hires you. And that's exactly what the PM Simulation Model is for."

"Und genau da setzen wir an… Und dafür braucht's im Grunde 2 Sachen…"

"So what you actually need is…" plus the three things, which is the system. Then "So let me show you how it works…" closes the loop opened at 0:30.

MechanismPhases in plain words

"Think of PM Simulations as a flight simulator for project managers… Phase 1 is people… Phase 2 is setting up the project… Phase 3 is planning it… Finally, Phase 4 is the actual execution."

"Intern nennen wir das unser Ugly Funnel System… und das läuft in 3 Phasen ab… Phase 1 ist Research… Phase 2 ist dann das Fundament… Erst wenn das alles passt, skalieren wir dann in Phase 3."

Three steps, in the order the ads use: we write and post, the media engine pushes, our team books. Concrete enough to picture: the one-hour sit-down, the post types, the 15 minutes, the calendar.

PayoffWhat it gets them, then a check

"And the people interviewing you are thinking… 'Wow… this person actually knows what they're talking about.'… See how much sense it makes?"

"Heißt, du verdienst an jedem Kunden deutlich mehr als deine Konkurrenz… und kannst das Budget hochdrehen, ohne dass dir die Marge wegbricht."

4.1 already has it: "by the time someone gets on a call with you… they already know, like, and trust your company."

Folded-in objectionsMore proof, doubts answered on the way

(Its own section) "'I work full-time and I have a family… do I even have time for this?' Most of my students do too…"

"Und dafür brauchst du auch kein riesiges Team… NoMisk hat das zum Beispiel mit weniger als 6 Leuten geschafft… Das klappt übrigens auch nicht nur bei Supplements…"

Iron's way, because it's shorter. No audience needed (Scott, from almost nothing). Any niche (Ryan, GovTech). Sounds like you (you sign off, 95% approved the first time). About 10 minutes a month.

HonestyLines that make the numbers believable

"Ich mach da auch überhaupt kein Geheimnis draus… das meiste davon zeig ich eh kostenlos auf YouTube. Aber wissen ist halt das eine… umsetzen das andere." · "Die Zahl hab ich mir auch nicht aus dem Arsch gezogen…"

Alex shares how LinkedIn works on his own LinkedIn. Knowing it and doing it every day are two different things, and that's the case for done for you. (Check that it's true for Alex.)

Working with usDeliverables in the order they happen

"We get on a 1-on-1 Blueprint Call… Every student gets their own senior project manager coach… every Saturday, I walk you through those projects live… we support you through your first 90 days…"

"[DELIVERABLES]… Und ganz wichtig… wir schalten nichts ab, was bei dir gerade funktioniert. Die neuen Funnels testen wir daneben…"

The onboarding hour, first posts live, the media engine on every post, calls booked into your calendar, a weekly report. Iron's safety line fits us exactly: nothing changes about how you sell now, this runs on top of your referrals.

Price rangeAfter the result, framed as a return

"Klar… günstig sind wir nicht. Je nach Umfang liegst du da irgendwo im 4 bis 5 stelligen Bereich im Monat… Aber wenn im Schnitt 1,4 Mio zusätzlich in den ersten 3 Monaten reinkommen… ist das eher ein No Brainer."

The same move, after the mechanism and never up front: "Now sure… we're not cheap…", the range, then what one new client is worth to them.

GuaranteeRisk reversal

"And if you don't land a project manager role within 2 months of finishing the training… we keep working with you until you do. That's the guarantee."

Still open with Alex. PM's version ("we keep working until we hit it", not "you don't pay") is one option to put to him.

Who it's forPush away to pull in

"If you're already a project manager and you just need a new resume… this isn't the right fit… And if things are tight financially right now… it's probably not the right time."

"Und falls ich dich damit gerade disqualifiziert hab… tut mir nicht leid… Wenn es nicht passt dann nehmen wir dich nicht… egal, wie viel du zahlen willst… Weil am Ende nämlich mein Name an jeder Brand hängt."

For: over a million a year, buyers on LinkedIn, will give one hour and take the calls. Not for: people who want followers or to go viral, want the cheapest writer, or won't sign off on posts.

CTAThe form, the call, the honest no

"Before the call, you'll answer a few quick questions… So I can look at everything beforehand… And if it's not the right fit… I'll tell you that honestly too… No hard feelings."

"Und selbst wenn wir am Ende nicht zusammenarbeiten… weißt du nach dem Call mit unserem Team trotzdem, wo es bei dir gerade hakt… Sebastian Szalinski, Iron Media… ich bin raus hier."

The form asks for the company, revenue, LinkedIn URL and who they sell to, so Alex has looked at their LinkedIn before the call. Either way they leave knowing why their LinkedIn isn't bringing in clients yet. The offer one last time. "Alex, Influx…"

The moves that make it work

The connectors that hand each section to the next

  • Offer → proof: "First off, my name's…" · "Also, kurz zu mir…"
  • Proof → soft CTA: "Now, if you've heard enough…" · "Und wenn dir das schon reicht…"
  • Soft CTA → situation: "Because chances are… your situation looks a lot like…"
  • Problem → mechanism: "THAT'S WHY… what you ACTUALLY need is…" · "Und genau da setzen wir an…"
  • Mechanism → case: "And I've seen that happen over and over again… Like with Tom."
  • Case → offer: "Which brings me to what working with me actually looks like." · "Und damit das bei dir genauso schnell geht…"
  • Offer → objections: "Now… I know this is a big decision."
  • → Who it's for: "So with all that being said… who is this actually for?"
  • → CTA: "So here's what to do now…" · "Und ob du das bist, finden wir im Call raus…"

What else repeats

  • A piece of proof roughly every 90 seconds: the opening stack, names in the mechanism, the case, an average result, a case from an unlikely niche.
  • Check-ins with the viewer: "You understand what I'm trying to say…?", "See how much sense it makes?"
  • CAPS on the turn, not on everything: "THAT'S WHY", "ACTUALLY", "BUT EVEN IF".
  • Questions in the viewer's own words, answered in a line or two.
  • Lines that admit the obvious: "Klar… günstig sind wir nicht."
  • The mechanism in phases, each with something you can picture (laptop, emails, RACI · advertorial, short page).
  • Same lingo as their ads: "…" at line ends, short lines, "And so on…", "Like think about it…"

The Influx structure

Eight sections, about 840 words, under 6 minutes at Alex's pace. The open loop gets closed in section 5.

1 · Offer0:00-0:35 · ~85 words. Callout, then the offer word for word from the ads, then "without ever writing a single post or stepping in front of a camera". Open loop: name the system, promise "exactly how that works, who I am, and what working with us looks like".
2 · Proof→1:15 · ~100. "First off, my name's Alex…" Top Voice twice, 60k+, his posts bring in over 7 figures a year. Influx: well over 8 figures for B2B clients. "But also founders who started right where you are…" Scott (almost nothing → [X] calls, [$X]). Ryan (GovTech, 79 → 1,059 → [X] calls).

5 · Mechanism→4:00 · ~180. "So let me show you how it works…" (closes the loop). 1: one hour with you, then we write and post every day in your voice, with a mix of posts for every stage, and you sign off in about 10 minutes a month. 2: the media engine, 20+ brands, 3M+ followers, 15 minutes, LinkedIn pushes it to your buyers. 3: our team books the ones who engage into your calendar. 7 · Who it's for→5:15 · ~60. Who it's for, who it isn't, and "we'd rather say no than take on a company where we can't hit the 30".
8 · CTA→5:45 · ~75. "So here's what to do now…" The form (company, revenue, LinkedIn URL, who you sell to). The call: Alex has already looked at your LinkedIn, maps out your 90 days, an honest no if it's not a fit, and you'll know why your LinkedIn isn't bringing in clients yet either way. The offer one last time. "Alex, Influx…"
