Before we write the VSL

The VSL is the ad they clicked, finished. Same Alex, same words, same offer, same proof.

Who we're talking to, what all 15 ads have already told them, what each ad promised when they clicked, and what the VSL has to repeat word for word. Plus the things an ad can leave open and a VSL can't.

15 ads, 6 ad groupsSource: Influx - Ad Scripts October 202615 Oct 2026

The short version

Eight things the VSL has to do so it feels like the same conversation as the ad.

  1. Pay off the click.All 15 CTAs end on "I'll show you how". 13 of 15 add "30 qualified calls in the next 90 days". The VSL starts by doing exactly that, inside the first minute.
  2. Alex, talking the way he does in the ads.Every ad is Alex, founder to founder: "And look…", "Like think about it…", "Now sure…". The old VSL reads like a briefing deck. People notice the switch.
  3. One offer, said one way.The ads word it at least five ways ("on the backend", "booked inbound calls", "into your calendar"). The VSL picks one and repeats it.
  4. Every belief, once more.Most viewers saw one or two ads, not fifteen. The VSL runs through the six ad-group beliefs again: no time, bad posts, referrals, they look you up, the media engine, the acquisition channel.
  5. The same steps, in the same order.One hour with you, we write and post every day in your voice, the media engine pushes every post, our team books the buyers who engage. Ten ads already say it this way.
  6. The same proof, in full.Alex's own LinkedIn, well over 8 figures for clients, Scott, Ryan. In an ad each one gets a line. In the VSL they get the before, the after and the real call and revenue numbers.
  7. Settle what the ads leave vague.Guarantee or not, who books the calls, what counts as a qualified call, price. An ad can skip them. The VSL can't, because the call depends on them.
  8. Don't reuse the old VSL's words."30 warm leads in 30 days or you don't pay" and "we can't promise meetings" contradict every ad. Parts of its structure can stay. The words and the offer can't.

Where the VSL sits

You said people get to the VSL after booking a call. The ads say "Click the link below and I'll show you how", and 4.1 adds "and you can book a call with me right there". That sounds like the video sits on the same page as the calendar. Which one it is changes what the VSL is for.

A · Before they book

Ad → VSL page with the calendar → booked call

  • Job: show them how, sell the call, keep out people who aren't a fit.
  • CTA: book a call with Alex.
  • Fits the ads as they're written. "I'll show you how" happens straight after the click.
  • The old funnel worked this way: 30in30.influxonline.co, then VSL → Typeform → Calendly → a confirmation page with a short Alex video.

B · After they book

Ad → booking page → booked → VSL → call

  • Job: get them to show up already sold. The how, the proof, the terms, the objections and what happens on the call, so Alex's call is about fit, not explaining.
  • CTA: show up, and put it in your calendar now.
  • Catch: the click then has to deliver "I'll show you how" on the booking page, or the ad CTAs change to "book a call".
Need from youA or B? If it's B, what's on the booking page: a headline, a short video, or only the calendar?

What wins with buyers this sophisticated

  • Not bigger numbers. Everyone claims reach.
  • The mechanism: we push every post and book the calls, not just write them.
  • The scoreboard: booked calls, not likes or views. 3.2 already says "Going viral on LinkedIn is THE worst thing".
  • Recognition: founder to founder, no hype, with Alex's own LinkedIn as the live example.

The congruency kit

What the VSL says the same way the ads do. If the ads already have a line for something, the VSL uses that line, not a new version of it.

The callout

"a B2B company doing over a million dollars a year" · "a 7 figure B2B company" · "over 7 figures a year"

Not "lean, ten to twenty people" or "founders and operators" (old VSL).

The offer

"build your LinkedIn brand… completely done for you… 30 qualified calls in the next 90 days"

Plus "without ever having to write a single post or step in front of a camera" (2.3, 4.1).

The system, as the ads already say it

  1. One hour"We sit down with you once for about an hour" (1.1, 5.1, 6.1) and get "your real stories… your opinions" (6.1).
  2. Write and post"every day, in your voice, about the stuff your buyers actually care about". Impact cards, infographics, long forms, case studies (3.1, 4.1, 5.2). A mix of top, middle and bottom of funnel posts (3.2).
  3. You sign off"nothing goes live until you've signed off on it". 95% approved the first time. About 10 minutes a month (4.2, 5.1, 6.1).
  4. Push"over 20 big LinkedIn brands… more than 3 million followers… react… in the first 15 minutes, so LinkedIn pushes it out to your buyers" (10 ads).
  5. Book"once they start engaging, our team books them straight into your calendar" (11 ads).

Worth keeping

The order of the sections as a starting point, until your guide replaces it. Who it's not for. What we do and what you do. Account safety. Honest urgency (limited capacity, no fake countdown). "I would rather a clean no than a wasted hour."

Has to go

The offer. The line that it can't promise meetings. The vocabulary. Lower thirds and on-screen cards written into the script (spoken lines only). The 13-minute length, until your guide sets it.

Rules that carry over from the ads

Everything from the ad training still applies. A 10-minute script has more places to break them.

  1. Flow first, then clarity, then length.The pen never leaves the paper. Each section hands off to the next on a connector, never a hard stop.
  2. "…" only at the end of a line.It carries a longer sentence onto the next line. A finished sentence gets a full stop.
  3. Easy words.If a 12-year-old wouldn't say it, simplify. Use the PM Revolution turns: "Now sure…", "I mean…", "Like think about it…", "So what are you supposed to do?".
  4. Banned."the catch is", "here's the thing", "warm leads", "the right people", "someone", vanity numbers like "potential clients showing interest". Said out loud it's "done for you", not "DFY".
  5. Proof in money terms."[X] new followers, [X] booked calls, [$X] in revenue". Engagement only backs that up.
  6. No bait, no invented details.They're sophisticated. Don't assume feelings they don't have, and don't make up stories, timelines or numbers.
  7. Spoken lines only.No [DRAW], [SHOW] or [ON SCREEN] in the script.
  8. Nothing against Meta.No story about Meta ads not working.
