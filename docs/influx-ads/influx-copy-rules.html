Influx · Meta ads · copy rules v3

How Influx ads should be written

Everything I pulled from your Meta training, your script-writing video, the Iron, Ecom MBA and PM scripts, and the Spoken Copy Playbook, turned into rules for Influx. I check every ad against this before I send it, and I update it after every round of your feedback.

Updated 12 Oct 2026Closest reference: Iron (also done-for-you)Iron lines translated from German

The short version

If an ad breaks one of these, it's not done.

  1. One ad, one job, one angle.The 3 creatives in a group are 3 different angles on the same job. Never two jobs in one creative.
  2. The words are the targeting.The first 2 lines make a B2B founder doing $1 to 10M raise a hand, and nobody else. "LinkedIn" alone pulls job seekers and coaches.
  3. We sell done-for-you, not a method.Open on their exact problem. Explain how it really works. Then "that's what we do for you at Influx".
  4. Flip a belief.Take something they believe, in their own words, and flip it. Agreeing with them is not a reframe.
  5. Teach one real thing.They should learn why it's happening and how it gets fixed. That's what proves we know what we're doing.
  6. Proof backs the flip.One line, right after the claim. Scott, Ryan, Alex's own numbers. Nothing made up.
  7. Never name the company.Explain the fix first. "We" only shows up at the very end, and not in every ad.
  8. The qualifier echoes the hook.B2B, revenue, already knows LinkedIn works, no time. Said plainly.
  9. The CTA means "we'll do this for you"."Watch how we'd run your LinkedIn for you" plus the outcome: 30 qualified calls booked in 90 days.
  10. Write it how Alex talks.Short lines, "…" for pauses, plain words. It reads top to bottom with no hard stops. About 200 to 245 words.

How the campaign thinks

This is why every ad has exactly one job. It comes straight from your training.

Nobody books off one ad

Meta's sequence learning watches what someone does with one ad and picks the next one to push them closer to booking. So we give it a team of 5 to 8 ads, each a different step, not 30 ads that all say "book a call".

The creative is the targeting

Meta reads every word and shows the ad to people like the ones it describes. Broad targeting, so the hook does the filtering.

The Influx ad shape

Iron's problem ads all follow the same 8 beats. They map onto SRPA: situation is beats 1 and 2, reframe is 3 and 4, proof is 5 and 6, ask is 7 and 8. The order is a starting point, and flow wins. Each beat has Sebastian's real line and the Influx version from my rebuilt 1.1.

1 · HookTheir situation in their words, with the ICP inside

"We just need better media buying." We hear that from almost every ecom brand at 100 to 500k a month… and it's almost never true.

"I'll start posting on LinkedIn properly… once things calm down." I hear that from almost every B2B founder doing 1 to 10 million a year… And things never calm down.

2 · What happenedConcrete, from their world, with one feeling

They have a few UGCs up front… a lightly optimized product page… and nothing at the back.

You probably posted 3 weeks in a row… Then a big client needed you… and it stopped. And now your last post is 4 months old.

3 · The flipThe real reason, which kills what they believe

Because Meta's AI made media buying so easy that pushing buttons just isn't a lever anymore.

But the problem was never your time… It's that every single post needed YOU.

4 · The fix, explainedTeach one real thing. Concrete enough to picture

Instead of sending everything to the product page, every awareness stage gets its own page… cold people get a long page that explains why they need it… warm people a short one.

The posts that work for founders are about how you see your industry… and that stuff doesn't change week to week. So you only need to get it out of your head once… and that's months of posts.

5 · We do itThe company, plus the part of the service that does this

We see this almost every day at my company Iron… which by the way scaled 30 brands to 8 figures this year alone.

That's how we do it at my company Influx. We spend one hour with you to get it all out… then my team writes your posts every week, in your voice.

6 · ProofOne line, right where it backs the claim

That's exactly how we took [CASE: Brand] from X a month to over X million a month.

That's how Scott, who runs an IT consulting firm, went from almost no following to 43 warm leads in the first month.

7 · QualifierWho's in, plus what they want

So… if you're doing between 100 and 500k a month and want to get to 8 figures without your margin breaking…

So if you run a B2B company doing over a million a year… you know LinkedIn works… you're just never gonna be the one writing the posts…

8 · CTA"How we'd do it for you", plus the outcome

Click the ad and see how we'd set up Ugly Funnels for your brand.

Click below and watch how we'd run your LinkedIn for you… and get you 30 qualified calls booked in the next 90 days.

Straight offer ads (AG4) are different

The hook is the offer. Offer plus open loop, then how we do it, then proof, then CTA plus the offer again.

The 5 tenets, every ad

  • Write first, edit after
  • Human filter: write how you'd say it
  • Rule of one: one angle, one job
  • Waterfall test: no hard stops out loud
  • Be honest: no overpromising

Hooks

The hook makes the right founder raise a hand. It doesn't need to entertain anyone.

  • B2B founder plus size or situation in the first 2 lines. "Too busy running the company" fits a plumber too.
  • Talk like an established company, not a creator. Clients, deals, sales calls, your team, 1 to 10 million a year. Never "personal brand", "go viral" or "grow your following". Those pull broke buyers.
  • Use their exact words from the calls. Mario's "90% referral", OJ's "important but not as important as the 10 other things I have to do", Mahdi's "LinkedIn has become full of very AI posts".
  • No setup lines. The hook flows straight into the point.
  • Every creative in a group gets a different hook type.

Real things we can teach

All true, all from Alex's own material.

  • Founder posts that work are about how you think, and that doesn't change week to week, so it can be captured once.
  • The first minutes after posting decide who sees a post.
  • Without a plan, posts get about 5 to 20 likes, mostly from your own team.
  • Reposts barely get any reach.
  • Company news gets ignored. "Nobody was responding because nobody had a reason to." (Scott case study)
  • Everyone has one post that randomly did well. What builds trust is showing up every week.
  • Buyers check your LinkedIn before they reply or book.
  • LinkedIn is the #2 most cited site in AI search answers, ahead of Wikipedia and YouTube (Semrush, an Adobe company, March 2026). Reshares are only about 5% of what gets cited.
  • Founders who post a lot still get nothing when every post is a repost or a pitch ("new feature", "speak to our team"). Nobody gets taken from "never heard of you" to "ready to talk". (Alex)

The whole thing in one breath (AG3)

Impact Cards get you seen. Thought leadership gets you trusted. Our network puts both in front of the buyers you want. And every week you get a list of who's already warm.

Qualifier + CTA

The qualifier says plainly who's in and echoes the hook. The CTA says what happens on click, in "we'll do it for you" words.

How the references qualify

  • Iron: "So… if you're doing between 100 and 500k a month and want to get to 8 figures…" (revenue plus outcome)
  • PM: "Now just so you know… this is only for people who've already spent at least 7 to 10+ years in another career."
  • Ecom MBA: "Let me be clear… this isn't a side hustle you start with a few hundred bucks."

What goes in an Influx qualifier

  • B2B, founder-led, over a million a year
  • Already knows LinkedIn works
  • Won't be the one doing it (no time)
  • For some ads: lives on referrals
  • Out: freelancers, solo consultants, real estate agents, job seekers, enterprise marketing teams

Writing for Alex's ear

  • One idea per line, "…" where Alex would pause, CAPS on the one word that carries the turn
  • Alex's fillers: "Look…", "candidly", "absolutely", "obviously". Nobody says "in today's landscape"
  • If you can explain it better in chat than in the script, the chat version goes in the script
  • Read it out loud. If Alex wouldn't say it on a sales call, rewrite it

Never do this

AI sales talk

  • "This isn't X where…" contrasts against a made-up category
  • "Not here." punchlines
  • Stacks of rhetorical questions (one pull question answered right away is fine)
  • "Everyone online says…" and generic reassurance
  • Announcer lines: "That's exactly what X gives you"
  • Clever tied-up endings and writerly words
  • Agreeing with the viewer instead of flipping something
  • Jumping from the problem straight to "that's what we do at Influx" (TV-ad pivot)
  • Analogies and dramatic lines ("a busy month killed it")
  • Two creatives in a group with the same opener, insight or Influx line

Influx rules

  • No em dashes
  • The only call number is the offer: 30 qualified booked calls in 90 days
  • No pipeline or revenue claims, no price
  • First names plus industry only, labelled "documented example"
  • Never describe the network engaging with posts ("our pages like your post", "they engage in the first 15 minutes"). Alex: in theory it isn't a practice LinkedIn supports. At most: "we have our own distribution on LinkedIn"
  • The AI voice setter isn't for sale. The human setter can be offered as an option
  • No system name at all. No "LQL", never read the URL out
  • No lingo that pulls broke buyers: "personal brand", "go viral", "grow your following", "side income", "content creator"

Plain, short and casual, in the copy and in chat. No smart-sounding names.

02

The ICP is already sold on LinkedIn. We don't sell them on LinkedIn, we sell them on not doing it themselves.

03

Angles come from what people really say on sales calls.

"Only referrals, and that's unreliable" is the bar. Real problems and real consequences, in their words.

04

In mechanism ads, every video explains the whole thing.

AG3 creatives each walk through the full system, from a different way in.

05

The offer creatives need angles that differ from each other.

4.1 is offer plus proof plus how. 4.2 and 4.3 can't be a rerun of it.

06

"The authority engine is not good… we dont sell info… in the beginning we talk about their exact thing… then we come in as the solution."

Open on their problem, explain how it really works, then "we do it for you at Influx". A name for the system is optional.

07

"Lets go 1 by 1."

One creative per message, with its goal and belief stated first.

08

"Your ad isn't following our best practices."

This page. Iron's done-for-you shape, a belief that gets flipped, one real thing taught, proof, the company named.

09

"30 qualified booked calls in 90 days is the offer. No system name."

Every CTA now ends on 30 qualified calls booked in 90 days. The "we do it" beat includes my team booking the calls. Nothing gets a name.

10

"Make sure we're messaging to the right type of ICP, no stupid lingo that attracts broke clients."

Talk like an established B2B company: clients, deals, sales calls, a million plus a year. No creator words.

11

"You got any proof this is happening from the sales calls?"

Only describe situations the calls actually show. "It's always the last thing on the list" is backed. "You posted 3 weeks then stopped" was me filling a gap.

12

"Don't use any of those cringe analogies, just very casually."

No snowballs, garages or shoemakers, and no dramatic lines like "a busy month killed it". Say it like you'd say it to a friend.

13

"Way too fast you come in, this sounds like straight up TV ad."

Never jump from the problem to "that's what we do at Influx". Bring Influx in as the person who sees this every day, then explain how we handle it for clients. The hard pitch lives in AG4.

14

"Sell on what LinkedIn can do for them, really quick, not too much."

One or two lines on the upside, like "the people you want as clients are on there every day". Then move on.

15

"We can't start each hook like that… we need more engaging hooks and also variety. Same with the reframe, each ad is genuinely unique."

Never the same opener twice in a group. Every creative gets its own hook type, its own insight, its own way of bringing in Influx, its own proof and its own CTA tie-back.

16

"Where's the info from why their methods didn't work?"

Every "why it didn't work" line has to come from Alex's material, the case studies or the calls. From the case studies: their posts were company news (reposts, announcements, hiring posts, webinar clips), and "nobody was responding because nobody had a reason to." The "LinkedIn shows it to a small group first" line was mine and is out.

17

"Too many ideas. Just say what they're doing wrong, then that's why no early engagement, no later push. Then the system that fixes it, especially the media thing."

One chain per ad: what they do wrong, why it kills the post, the fix. 1.2 is allowed to describe the big pages reacting in the first 15 minutes (flag to Alex).

18

"Don't mention Influx. Mention the fix and then at the very end, even if then not every time."

No company name in ads. The fix gets explained on its own; "we" only comes in at the end with the proof or the CTA.

19

Heik's rewrite of 1.2 (v9).

The fix mirrors the problem chain one-to-one: nobody cares, so we write posts they care about; nobody reacts, so they get early reactions. The fix is tied to their goal right away ("so you can get qualified calls coming in"). The one unique piece gets a casual name where it's explained ("I call this the media engine"); the offer as a whole stays unnamed. The proof says plainly that we did it ("we wrote and published"). The CTA promises two layers: grow your LinkedIn brand, AND 30 qualified calls on the backend. No hedges like "or so".

20

"Fuck your hooks that you always start with quotes."

Quote hooks are out as a default. Use a question, a sweeping claim, a "show this", a hard statement or a scenario instead, and vary them across the group.

21

"Use unique hooks… look at the variety here, all your ads sound like the same bullshit." (Iron v11)

Every creative needs its own hook type AND its own body shape (staircase, story, teaching, rant, numbers) AND its own qualifier and CTA tie-back. Hook bank from Iron: most common reason, "usually it's…" pattern, polarity ("makes some rich and others broke"), "everyone knows X, but the funny thing is…", fun fact, "real talk… everyone talks about X but nobody does it", quote + flip, "stop doing X", "I find it funny how…", real question with a number, calling out the industry, humour flex, "I'll be honest with you…", "if I were in your shoes…", "I have a big problem with…". Used so far: 1.1 callout, 1.2 most common reason, 1.3 "every time I talk to founders", 2.1 "everyone has the same plan".

22

"Here's the kinda vibe I was going for… don't fuck up my conversational flow." (Heik's own 2.3)

Say the consequence plainly and make them picture it: "Now… think about what's going to happen if there's barely anything there. It's going to be very hard for you to actually close them. And not only that… they'd probably even go with your competitor." Then land the point ("So yes… X really is THAT important if you want…"), concede their reality ("But sure… you're busy…"), and step in ("And that's where we step in."). Mine was abstract ("one call usually isn't enough to hand someone a big contract"). When Heik hands over a draft: fix spelling and capitals, fill the gaps with placeholders, and leave every line and pause where he put it.

23

"Fuck warm leads."

"Warm leads" is lead-gen lingo. The offer is calls, so the result line says "booked [X] qualified calls". For the case-study numbers (Scott 43, Ryan 61), say "potential clients showing interest". They count comments with buying intent plus clicks, so never turn them into calls.

24

"Now do a full analysis… this should be ad training for you." (Heik's final 2.3)

Full breakdown in influx-ad-training.html. In short: say the cost in one plain line and stack it ("And not only that…"), land the point ("So yes… THAT important if you want…"), agree with the excuse ("But sure…"), step in, introduce Alex with the biggest sourced result, give the how real numbers (daily, 20+ brands, 3M+ followers, 15 minutes), qualify on what they want, plain CTA. Never "the right people". Never reuse the last ad's body.

25

"Over a million dollars brother, every time." (3.1)

Never "over a million a year". Always "over a million dollars a year", or "a 7 figure business" for variety.

26

"Remove these things from each ad." (on-screen qualified-call line)

No "Qualified call = [definition]" on screen in any ad. AG3 and AG4 lessons are in influx-ad-training.html: proof means money, question hooks only if they really ask it, full sentences, done-for-you in every ad, never "someone".

27

"Here's also how I changed the CTA." (3.1 v4)

New default CTA: "Click below and I'll show you how we'd build and run your whole LinkedIn brand completely done for you… And get you 30 qualified booked inbound calls in the next 90 days on the backend." Spoken as "done for you", not "DFY".

28

"Fuck all of these instructions, how often do I have to tell you that." (3.2)

Ads are spoken lines only. No [DRAW], [SHOW] or [ON SCREEN] directions, ever. Open items go in one list at the top of the scripts file, never inside an ad.

29

"This is what I did, iterate your learning again." (Heik's final 3.2)

Once the point lands, end fast: hand off on the word they just heard ("calls… And that's exactly what we'll do for you at my company Influx"), state the offer, then "Click the link below and I'll show you how." No new mechanism pieces after the point, no second qualifier. Loop the ending back to the hook. One mechanism per AG3 ad.
