Before we write the pre-call emails

Every email has one job: get a 7 figure founder to show up already sold, in the same words as the ad that got them to book.

What the sequence is for, what your show-rate video says it has to do, how the Ecom MBA and App Accelerator sequences are built, the ICP again from the sales calls, and every objection mapped to where it's already answered and what the emails still have to do.

Refs: Ecom MBA (15 emails), App Accelerator (18)ICP: 9 sales calls + Tony, Mario, Shikha26 Oct 2026

The short version

  1. Two jobs, in this order.First get them to the call (show rate). Then make the call a formality (every question answered, the proof seen, the price not a shock).
  2. One email, one job.Each email takes one question or one proof point and answers it fully. Both reference sequences work this way.
  3. Same offer, same words, all the way down.Your video: one email that talks to the wrong person and a 7 figure founder decides "this isn't for me" and no-shows.
  4. Alex writing to a peer.Plain text, from Alex, signed "- Alex". Same line style as the ads, but calmer: less hype, more "here's exactly how it works".
  5. Every email ends at the call."On the call, we'll…" with something specific to their company, every single time.
  6. B2B founders check you out.They look Alex up, compare vendors and pull in finance before they buy. The emails should hand them what they'd go looking for anyway.

Job 1 · Show rate

  • Lock the call in: the invite accepted, in the calendar they actually use
  • Keep Influx top of mind every day until the call
  • Get a reply early (the engager email), which also helps the emails land in the inbox
  • Remind them why they booked, in the offer's own words

Job 2 · Pre-sell

  • Answer every question they'd otherwise ask on the call, or not ask and just not buy
  • Show proof they can check: Alex's LinkedIn, client results, the case studies
  • Make the price and the terms no surprise
  • Give them what they need to bring finance or a co-founder along

Your video: the two levers

"Why you're struggling with low show rates (it's NOT your pre-call nurture systems)." Both levers sit before the emails, but they decide what the emails are allowed to say.

Lever 1 · Ads messaging and lead intent

Meta matches the ad's words to the people likely to act on them, so talk only to the ICP. Go after solution-aware leads, the ones already in the field who only need to be sold on your mechanism and on you. They understand what fixing the problem is worth, so they show up and they pay.

For Influx that's already the brief: founders of 7 figure B2B companies who are "already sold on LinkedIn" (your ICP line). The emails never re-sell LinkedIn. They sell the Authority Engine, and Alex.

Lever 2 · Front end and back end congruency

The message has to stay the same from the ad to the closing call: VSL, emails, thank-you pages, SMS, setters, sales assets. Your example: a $1M ARR founder books, then opens one email about "0 to $10k MRR", decides "this isn't for me" and cancels.

For Influx: every email talks to a founder running a 7 or 8 figure B2B company, with the same offer (LinkedIn brand, 30 qualified calls in 90 days, done for you), the same mechanism names and the same proof as the ads and the VSL.

Anatomy of one email

SubjectLower case, personal, specific. Their question ("Is this really a 1on1 program?"), the promise ("I'll keep working with you until you hit $30K"), a curiosity hook ("the $500k mistake I'll make sure you never make") or proof ("my results (screenshots inside)").
Opener"Hey {{first_name}}," then straight into the point. Often the answer comes first: "Quick answer: Yes, the app accelerator is 1on1." / "You don't need to quit your job or step back from your business…"
Why it comes up"I'm bringing it up because people ask me on almost every call…" It tells them they're normal for wondering.
The answerThe real answer with specifics: numbers, steps, what's included and what isn't.
ProofA personal story ("I over-ordered and ended up with $500,000 worth of inventory"), a named client, a screenshot.
Honest line"Now, I'll be straight with you, none of this happens overnight…" / "Angry Orange was an agency client, not someone from the program…"
Close"On your discovery call, we'll…" plus something specific to them. "See you there." "- Adam"
P.S.Optional: a link to go deeper, or the guarantee again.

Length runs from one line (the engager) to about 400 words (how it works). Most are 150 to 300. The line style is the ads': one short line per paragraph, "…" at line ends, spoken turns like "Now, just so we're clear…", "Anyway…", "I'll be honest…".

What's in their head between booking and the call

They booked from a VSL that answered most of it. Over the next few days, the doubts that are left come back, and their week fills up.

"Is this worth an hour?"A 7 figure founder's calendar fills up. If nothing reminds them why they booked, the call is the first thing to go.
"Is this just a pitch?"They get "20, 40 a day" LinkedIn pitches (OJ). The gameplan email has to say what they get from the call even if they don't buy.
"Can I trust them?"They'll look Alex up. Shikha: "can I trust you guys to bring me sales". Give them the proof before they go looking.
"Does it fit us?"Their industry, their size, their ICP being on LinkedIn, someone on the team already posting.
"What will it cost, and can I justify it?"To themselves, and to finance or a co-founder.
"Who else should be on this?"If finance or a co-founder decides with them, getting them on the first call saves a second one.

Congruency kit for the emails

Say it the same way

  • Who: "B2B founders doing over a million dollars a year" · "7 and 8 figure B2B founders"
  • Offer: "build your LinkedIn brand… THE market authority in your niche… 30 qualified calls in the next 90 days… completely done for you"
  • Mechanism: the Authority Engine · top, middle and bottom of funnel content · the media engine (20+ big LinkedIn brands, 3M+ followers, within the first 15 minutes) · SDRs in your DMs
  • Their time: one hour to onboard, about 10 minutes a month, no writing, no camera
  • Price: mid to high four figures a month depending on scope
  • Guarantee: "If we don't book you 30 qualified calls in the next 90 days… we keep working for you, at no extra cost, until we do"

Never

  • Talk to anyone smaller or newer ("grow your first audience", "start your personal brand")
  • Re-sell LinkedIn itself. They're already sold
  • "buyers", "the right people", "here's the thing", "the catch is", "I'm not guessing", stacked fragments
  • Follower counts or engagement as the result (only as the before)
  • Fake urgency or fake countdowns. Alex's own line: "I am not going to invent a fake countdown"
  • "Program", "students", "course". Influx is done-for-you, not coaching

Open before writing

Need from you or Alex
  • How far out calls usually get booked. That decides how many emails go out and how often (two a day if the call is tomorrow, one a day if it's a week out).
  • Which numbers are the source of truth: Alex's V2 ads have real results (Scott 4,000 followers, 34 calls, $120,000 in 12 weeks; Ryan 37 calls, $245,000; Devon, John, Colin, Jane). The October doc you just sent still has [X]. And "Tony" in V2 5.2 needs sorting out.
  • Assets we can link: Alex's LinkedIn, client posts we can screenshot, the case study PDFs, any video or podcast of Alex.
  • The same Alex sign-offs as the VSL: guarantee terms, what counts as a qualified call, "SDRs in your DMs", the price range in writing.
  • Sender name and address (Alex's own inbox makes the engager email work).
