Influx · Internal offer breakdown

What Influx sells, how it works, and what it costs

Influx installs and runs a done-for-you LinkedIn Authority System on a founder's profile. It turns their expertise into content, pushes that content in front of their ideal buyers through an owned distribution network, and hands back a list of warm, ICP-qualified people the founder can pursue. The Meta offer wraps all of it in one promise: 30 warm ICP leads in 30 days, or you don't pay.

In plain terms

Influx is the LinkedIn authority brand of Fetched L.L.C-FZ, a Dubai company founded by Alex Alleyne (influxonline.co). It works on one platform only. Alex calls the category "a hybrid between a lead generation agency and a PR agency": it raises the founder's profile the way PR does, and it is judged on leads the way lead gen is.

What it is

  • A done-for-you system installed on the founder's personal LinkedIn profile, with the company page as an optional second "brand".
  • Strategy, writing, design, publishing, distribution and reporting, all run by Influx.
  • Built from one recorded interview, then approved by the founder before anything goes live.
  • Measured on warm, ICP-qualified leads. Likes and impressions are supporting signals, not the product.

What it is not

  • Not a content calendar or writing tool the founder has to operate.
  • Not an AI writer. The VSL says it plainly: "If you want a thirty-five dollar AI writer, we are the wrong firm."
  • Not appointment setting. Influx does not book calls or meetings.
  • Not paid LinkedIn ads. There is no ad budget on top of the fee.
  • Not video or podcast production, and not coaching.

The 30-in-30 promise

This is the centrepiece of the Meta funnel. It appears in the VSL, the brief and the newer discovery calls. Deck V3 does not mention it.

30 warm, ICP-qualified LinkedIn leads in 30 days, or you don't pay.

Paired in the VSL with a second promise of equal weight: "we protect your time." About one hour to onboard, then approvals.

What counts as a lead

From the brief: "A person who matches the client's agreed ICP (role, seniority, company type and size) and who has shown engagement or intent with the client's LinkedIn presence, for example engaging with content, viewing the profile, or interacting with the client's activity."

So every lead passes two tests: they fit the ICP agreed at onboarding, and they gave a signal. A profile view counts.

What the client receives

"Intent and lead data: a list of warm ICP-qualified people the client can act on."

  • Month 1: one list for the month.
  • After month 1: a new list every week.
  • Each person is scored, with a percentage match to the ICP and the reason they were picked (Alex, Shikha call).

What is not promised

  • Booked meetings or sales calls.
  • Closed revenue. The VSL: "We will not pretend LinkedIn closes a six-month deal in week three."
  • Results identical to the case studies.

LQL vs warm ICP lead

Older calls use "LQL" (LinkedIn Qualified Lead), counted from comments showing intent and clicks on tracked links. DMs only counted if the client tallied them. The 30-in-30 definition is broader because it includes profile views. Use "warm ICP lead" in anything public. LQL is internal jargon.

The system

Three named parts. The Authority Engine is the whole done-for-you system. The Media Engine is the distribution layer inside it. The intent scoring layer turns engagement into the lead list. Alex said on 30 Sep that it was "built very recently", after the case studies ran. It has no public name yet.

  1. Founder expertiseCaptured once in a 1-hour interview: voice, ICP, offer, stories, beliefs.
  2. Narrative strategyPositioning and content pillars tied to the commercial conversations the founder wants.
  3. Authority contentImpact Cards and long-form posts written in the founder's voice and approved by them.
  4. Media EngineEarly engagement tells LinkedIn the post is relevant. The algorithm then shows it to matching buyers.
  5. Buyer trustICP buyers see useful, consistent thinking and start engaging before any sales conversation.
  6. Warm ICP leadsSignals are scored against the ICP and delivered as a list.
  7. Client follows upInflux stops at warm interest. The client's team turns it into conversations.

The Media Engine, public version

"The Media Engine creates the early relevance signals authority content needs. Once the authority narrative is clear, targeted distribution helps content reach the right audiences faster and earn wider visibility signals." (Deck V3)

Alex's metaphors on calls: getting a snowball down a hill, or jump-starting a car. "Difficult up front. Once it has momentum, it compounds."

The Media Engine, as described on calls internal

  • 20+ owned theme company pages (AI, sales, business insights, a general "daily wisdom" page), roughly 200k to 300k followers each, 3M+ in total, mostly US and Europe.
  • A separate pool of "notable creator" profiles.
  • Pages leave likes only, "so it doesn't appear bot-like". Creators leave comments.
  • Engagement lands within about 15 minutes of a scheduled post.
  • Replaces boosting. There is no paid ad spend.
Keep this out of ads

Deck V3 says "All engagement to your profile is organic and authentic." That sits awkwardly next to the internal description above. In public copy, describe distribution at deck level ("targeted distribution creates early relevance signals"). Never describe the page network and never claim "100% organic".

Reporting

A report every Tuesday with a day-by-day breakdown of the previous week: visibility, ICP exposure, engagement and inbound indicators, plus the plan for the coming week. The warm lead list sits alongside it. Month 1 is a testing phase against a hypothesis. From day 31 the data decides what gets scaled and what gets dropped.

Content and voice

Influx reuses formats it has already tested instead of inventing new ones per client. In Alex's words, "consistent proven formats versus innovating for the sake of it."

Impact Cards

Visual, statement-based posts. The most consistent performer. In the Scott case they were built to "solve immediate technical friction" for buyers and drive top-of-funnel reach.

Thought leadership

Longer-form posts that build trust and depth, or drive a specific call to action. In the Scott case they "challenged conventional industry wisdom".

Newsletter add-on

Sold separately, mainly to grow visibility in AI search. Both featured clients added one after starting.

Cadence and topics

  • Standard: Monday to Friday, one post a day, about 20 a month. Alex told Shikha "at least five times a week".
  • Topics are mostly evergreen: beliefs, leadership principles, stories, operating experience. That is what keeps approvals to about 10 minutes a month.
  • If priorities or perspective shift, Influx runs another capture call.
  • Company page and founder publish different posts with connected themes. Reposting gets suppressed by the algorithm.

Voice standards (Deck V3)

  • Deep understanding, not invention. Real operating experience, extracted in a structured onboarding.
  • Zero AI-speak. Written to the founder's natural cadence, without buzzwords or corporate jargon.
  • Total editorial control. Nothing publishes without factual review and voice approval.
  • First-time approval rate: 90% (Deck V3) or 95% (brief).

How it runs

Live in about a week, assuming approvals come back on time. Two approval gates happen before anything is published. After that the founder approves once a month.

  1. Day 0
    Decision

    Scope chosen, access permissions set up.

  2. Days 1–2
    Onboarding

    The 1-hour interview. Voice of the Customer document within 48 hours.

  3. Days 3–5
    Engine build

    Strategy, roadmap, content showcase, distribution set-up.

  4. Days 6–7
    Activation

    First post goes live.

  5. Days 8–30
    Launch phase

    Test the hypothesis, collect data.

  6. Day 31+
    Scale phase

    Double down on what works, drop what doesn't.

The two approval gates

  • Gate 1: Voice of the Customer document. Influx's read on how the founder thinks, acts and operates, plus the strategy.
  • Gate 2: Content showcase. A sample set of the posts that will go live first.

"Nothing goes live without your sign-off."

The monthly rhythm

  • Start of month: 30 days of content arrives for one approval pass.
  • Every Tuesday: the weekly report.
  • From month 2: a weekly warm lead list.
  • Some clients stop approving once trust is built, and Influx simply publishes (Alex, Marigold call).

Who does what

The time-protection promise depends on this split. The client's real job after onboarding is approvals, plus following up the leads.

Influx owns

  • Positioning strategy and the founder narrative
  • Content production: writing, editing, design
  • Publishing on schedule
  • Media Engine distribution
  • Weekly reporting every Tuesday
  • Scoring and delivering the warm lead list

The client owns

  • Subject-matter insight: one hour at onboarding
  • Voice approval: two gates up front, then a monthly review
  • Flagging any change in strategic priorities
  • Following up the leads. Influx cannot see DMs and does not book meetings. It offers guidance, and a managed follow-up service exists for some clients.
Account access

Everything runs through a secure, encrypted third-party platform with permission boundaries. Influx never needs the founder's password. "Zero account bans since inception."

Scope options

Pricing is per "brand". One brand is either one person's profile or one company page. Alex's recommended minimum is one founder plus the company page, or two executives, to get a "flywheel effect".

Executive Authority

One founder, CEO or senior exec. A focused build around the main commercial voice. The likely Meta entry point.

Personal + Company Brand recommended

Connects the founder's authority to company credibility, category trust and demand capture. Two brands.

Leadership Team Expansion

Several executives visible at once. Usually where accounts grow after a successful start ("land and expand").

A separate product: Media Engine only not the Meta offer

For teams that write their own content and only want amplification (sold to a well-funded AI scale-up in August). Two tiers: Growth uses part of the page network, roughly 5x. Accelerator uses the full network plus creator comments, roughly 10x. The standard volume is Monday to Friday, 20 posts a month. Priced per scope.

Copy rules

Hard rules from the brief and the VSL production notes. They apply to every ad, page and script.

Never

  • Em dashes in client-facing copy
  • Claim Influx books calls or meetings
  • Surnames, company names or client photos in case studies
  • "$50k pipeline", impression guarantees, or invented Media Engine figures
  • Read the calendar URL on camera
  • Hype slogans

Always

  • Label results "documented example", never a promise of identical results
  • Define "lead" on screen whenever the 30-in-30 is stated
  • Use anonymised first names: "Ryan, GovTech" and "Scott, IT consulting"
  • Say "book a call" or "book a Discovery call"
  • Speak peer to peer: direct, commercial, in complete sentences
  • Treat time protection as equal to the lead promise
