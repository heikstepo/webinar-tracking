## 1. Company and Offer Brief: what's new or overrides batch 1

### Company
- Influx is the LinkedIn Authority brand of Fetched L.L.C-FZ (Dubai). Site: influxonline.co.
- Offer statement: a "DFY LinkedIn Authority Engine for lean B2B founders, CEOs and executives who need their LinkedIn presence to produce pipeline, not vanity metrics."
- It is installed on the client's **personal profile** so "the founder stops being the content bottleneck."

### Alex bio (new)
- 2x LinkedIn Top Voice.
- About 60k LinkedIn followers.
- **Former EVP at Brace, where he led an 8-figure team.**
- Runs every sales call personally and closes every deal.

### Process as the brief states it (use these numbers in ads)
1. Onboard: **1 hour max.** Voice, ICP, offer, stories.
2. Produce: written in the client's voice, **95% first-time approval.** (The calls said 90%, so the brief wins.)
3. Approve: **about 10 minutes a month.**
4. Distribute: the Media Engine publishes and amplifies to the ICP. No password needed, zero account bans.
5. Report: **a weekly list of warm, ICP-qualified leads.**

### 30-in-30 definition (now resolved)
- Promise: 30 warm, ICP-qualified LinkedIn leads in 30 days, or you do not pay.
- **Lead** = a person who matches the client's agreed ICP (role, seniority, company type and size) AND has shown engagement or intent with the client's LinkedIn presence. Examples: engaging with content, **viewing the profile**, or interacting with the client's activity.
- This is broader than the LQL definition on the calls, which was comments with intent plus UTM clicks.
- The deliverable is **"intent and lead data: a list of warm ICP-qualified people the client can act on."**
- Never claim Influx books calls or meetings.

### Pricing (internal, not for ads)

| Plan | Price | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| 12-month Authority Engine | $3,900 per month per brand | 12-month agreement |
| 3-month Proof of Value | $4,900 per month | Paid upfront, "about $14,670" |

Note: 3 x $4,900 = $14,700. Check the "about $14,670" figure.

### ICP (confirmed)
- SMB and young mid-market B2B.
- About $1-10M revenue. The Typeform gates out anyone below about $500k.
- About 10-20 employees.
- Founder-led: the founder, CEO or senior exec is **both the face and the closer.**
- Lean, no time for content.
- Direction is away from enterprise.

### Copy rules (hard)
- No em dashes.
- Never claim Influx books Strategy Calls or meetings.
- Case studies use anonymized **first names only**: "Scott, IT consulting" and "Ryan, GovTech". No surnames, company names or client photos.

### Funnel and media (live since 05 Oct 2026)
- Meta ABO test, **US, ages 25-64, broad**, about $225/day.
- Path: 30in30.influxonline.co, with VSL → Typeform qualifier → Calendly Discovery → confirmation page with an Alex video.
- **Implication:** with broad targeting, the creative has to do the audience selection. ICP callouts in the hooks are mandatory, not optional.

### Capacity
- About 15 brands under management.
- Omar runs fulfilment. The brief calls capacity "elastic, can absorb new clients."
- This conflicts with the VSL's "limited installs, finite capacity on purpose." Keep any scarcity soft and honest.

## 4. Gary King (Cidrines bakery): voice of customer
Off-ICP: CPG, 48-year-old family bakery, selling into retail and now food service. The psychology still transfers.

**Outreach hook that worked**
- "She sent this thing telling me how bad I sucked at LinkedIn."
- The visibility report and videos got the meeting. This is a possible Meta lead magnet or angle: a "visibility gap" audit.

**Invisible / mute**
- "we're pretty much non-existent"
- "mostly mute... since our inception"
- The founders are "very humble... it's all about the product"

**No time (and he knows it's an excuse)**
- "I can use the excuse that you hear from every other person. Don't have a lot of time."

**"Who cares what I think"**
- "even when I think something's interesting, it's interesting maybe in the bakery world, but not more broadly"

**Envy of a consistent peer** (strong angle)
- "he's posting three or four times a week... I admire the hell out of him. I don't know where he gets the time."

**Doesn't want to be an influencer**
- "more interested in how we tell the company story than elevating my own whatever. I'm an old guy."

**LinkedIn cynicism**
- "where people go when they're looking for a job or... looking for their next client"
- "only things I get in my inbox right now are people trying to sell stuff"

**Wants proof**
- "do you have success stories that this has elevated companies?"

**ROI is hard to prove versus known channels**
- Trade marketing at H-E-B is a "predictable 4x." LinkedIn is "a little trickier, longer runway."
- Board approval is needed: "a little pool of money for year one, it's going to have to really show something."

**Small-company price fear**
- "if you're going to come and say this is a multi-million dollar package, we're not going to be in"
- "good, better, best menu"
- "you're not going to become independently wealthy with this year one"

**Repost myth** (educational hook)
- Gary assumed he would just repost the company page.
- Alex: reposts get suppressed. Exec and page should both post unique, thematically linked content.

## 5. HeyGen: Media Engine mechanics (internal only)
- An amplification-only deal. HeyGen writes its own content, and Influx amplifies within 15 minutes of each post going live.
- **Media Engine:**
  - 20+ owned theme company pages (AI, business insights, sales, "daily wisdom", similar to Daily Sales).
  - 3M+ total followers, mostly US and Europe.
  - A separate pool of "notable creator" profiles.
  - Pages leave likes only, "so it doesn't appear bot-like." Creators leave comments.
- **Tiers:** Growth (part of the page pool, about 5x) and Accelerator (the full pool, about 10x).
- **Standard volume:** Monday-Friday, 1 post a day, 20 posts a month.
- **Ad copy risk:** describing the page network in ads ("our 3M-follower pages like your post") reads as engagement-pod or inauthentic behaviour. That is a reputational risk and a possible ad-review issue. Stay at deck level: "targeted distribution that creates early relevance signals." Alex's **snowball** and **jump-start** metaphors are safe and vivid.
- The buyer language here ("army of micro-influencers", "play the algorithm") belongs to a scale-up marketing team, not the founder ICP. Don't use it.
