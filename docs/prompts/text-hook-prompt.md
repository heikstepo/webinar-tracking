# Prompt: on-screen text hooks for any offer

10 Nov 2026. Built from Heik's feedback on the Influx text hooks (copy rules 152-155). Paste it into a new chat with the ad scripts.

```
You're writing the on-screen text hooks for a set of video ads. A text hook is the short line on screen during the first seconds of the video, while the person on camera says the spoken hook.

Its job is to hook people a second way. It never repeats what's being said in the first 3 seconds. It adds something on top of it, and it's still clearly about this exact ad.

INPUTS
Ad scripts (each creative has a number and a title):
[PASTE AD SCRIPTS]

Offer and ICP (if this is empty, work them out from the scripts):
[OFFER + ICP: who it's for, their situation, their main blocker, what they already believe, what they've tried before]

Reference text hooks from another niche (optional, use their length and logic, never their words):
[PASTE REFERENCE TEXT HOOKS]

HOW TO WRITE EACH ONE
1. Read the whole ad. Note the spoken hook (roughly the first 3 seconds), the angle of the ad, and anything it reveals later (a quote, a stat, a story, a reason, a result).
2. Give the text hook a different job from the spoken hook:
   - If the spoken hook is a story or a claim, the text calls out the viewer's situation.
   - If the spoken hook is a callout or the offer, the text adds the payoff, a credential, how little effort it takes, or curiosity.
3. Lean on curiosity. The best text hooks open a loop the ad closes a few seconds later. Example: the ad quotes a founder later, so the text hook teases that quote.
4. Write at least 3 different angles in your head, then keep the strongest one. A different angle means a different idea, not the same line reworded.

RULES FOR EVERY TEXT HOOK
- Tied to the ICP. Every hook names a piece of the ICP: who they are, their situation, their blocker, what they believe, or what they've tried and got nothing from.
- Keywords. Every hook carries the platform or category keyword (for example "LinkedIn", "TikTok Shop", "Amazon") or a clear ICP keyword (for example "B2B founders", "referrals", "booked calls"). A line that would fit any business is wrong.
- Not mostly questions. Mix the formats: a plain statement, a callout at the start ("B2B founders: …"), "X vs. Y", "A → B → C", "warning: before you…", "the [thing] that…:", "(nobody shows you this)". A question is fine now and then, never as the default.
- Short and punchy. 4 to 11 words. Easy, spoken words. No jargon, no clever wordplay that needs a second read.
- Different from each other. Don't use the same format or angle twice in a row, and don't reuse another ad's angle.
- Safe for Meta:
  - No income or money claims, no dollar figures, no "7/8 figures".
  - No "guaranteed".
  - No promise of a result for the viewer, and nothing that implies they'll get the same results as a client or the founder.
  - Only use facts, numbers and stats that are in the script. Quote a stat exactly: if it says "social media", don't change it to a platform name.
  - Leave out anything the client has asked you not to mention.

CALIBRATION (from a different offer: copy the logic, not the words)
- Repeats the spoken hook (bad): "What prospects see when they check your LinkedIn before a call", over a spoken hook about prospects looking you up before a call.
  Better: "Your LinkedIn pitches before you do"
- Missing the keyword (bad): "hired a ghostwriter and still no new clients?"
  Better: "hired a LinkedIn ghostwriter and still no booked calls?"
- A fear question where curiosity works better (bad): "What happens when your referrals slow down?"
  Better, teasing a quote the ad gives later: "What a founder with 90% referrals told me:"
- Repeats the offer the founder is saying (bad): "B2B founders: your LinkedIn brand, done for you"
  Better: "B2B founders: stop writing your own LinkedIn posts"
- Punchy and easy (good): "No time for LinkedIn? We post for you."

SELF-CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
For each hook, check all of these. Rewrite any that fail.
- Does it avoid repeating the first 3 seconds?
- Does it add a second reason to keep watching?
- Is it tied to this exact ad?
- Does it name part of the ICP?
- Does it have the keyword?
- Is it 4 to 11 words?
- Is it safe?
- Could it run on any other business's ad? If yes, it fails.

OUTPUT
Only the list, exactly in this format, with the creative's number and title exactly as they appear in the doc, and a blank line between each:

1.1 [Title as in the doc]: [Text hook]

1.2 [Title as in the doc]: [Text hook]

No explanations, no extra options, no commentary.

WHEN I GIVE FEEDBACK
- "redo [number]": give a new angle for that one, not the same idea reworded.
- "[number], other angle" or "more curiosity" or "not question based": change the angle or format the way I asked.
- "give me X options": give X genuinely different angles for that one, numbered, one line each.
- "keep [numbers]": leave those exactly as they are, redo all the others, and give me the full list again in the same format.
```
