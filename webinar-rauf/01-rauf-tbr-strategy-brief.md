## 3. The system, A→Z

Four layers, always in this order. Layers 2–4 are what separates his framework from "a range
indicator" — and the content section has to teach all four or the mechanism looks thin.

### Layer 1 — TIME: the ranges themselves

Two windows, **Eastern Time**, every day:

| Session | TBR window | Catalyst / trade window |
|---|---|---|
| London | **1:12 – 2:12 am ET** | ~3:00 am ET |
| New York | **8:12 – 9:12 am ET** | **9:30 – 10:00 am ET** |

Mechanics:
1. Mark the **high and the low** of that 60-minute window. That's the range.
2. Wait for **one side to be swept**.
3. Target the **opposing end** — or equilibrium (50%), which carries a higher win rate.
4. **No trades before the window closes. No trades immediately after it closes. Wait for the open.**
   For New York that means nothing happens until 9:30.
5. If no setup by 10:00 am, there is no trade. Walk away.
6. Max **2 trades per session** (4/day if trading both). If the first loses, re-evaluate and
   re-enter in the *same direction* — "if they're both wrong, then you're just wrong on the day."

The 9:30 open is explicitly framed as **manipulation by design**: *"Always view the 9:30 open as a
manipulation. Its job is to take out liquidity above highs and lows where orders can get trapped."*

Why 8:12 and not 8:00? He calls them "time cycles to the market" and never explains the derivation
publicly. **We need the why from him** — a webinar audience will ask, and "because it works" is the
weakest possible answer for the paradigm slot. He also strongly implies the academy contains
**additional ranges** beyond these two ("more access to ranges").

### Layer 2 — BIAS: the hourly candle

*"What if you treated your hourly time frames as your daily time frames? A whole new world is going
to open up to you."*

- London bias candle = the **1:00 am** hourly candle. New York = the **8:00 am** hourly candle.
- If the next candle (2am / 9am) **breaks the low** → bias is **bullish**, target that candle's high.
- If it **breaks the high** → bias is **bearish**, target that candle's low.
- If it breaks **neither** (indecisive candle) → **no trade**, market consolidates, go do something else.
- **The three-candle swing point:** candle 1 sets the extreme; candle 2 sweeps it and *closes back
  inside candle 1's range*; candle 3 expands to the opposite side. That's a confirmed swing high/low
  and it is his highest-conviction bias signal.
- Micro-timing inside any hourly candle: **minutes 12–20 = manipulation; minute 30 → close =
  distribution.** (This is the hidden logic behind the :12 start.)
- Extra: if the 9am candle is one-sided for its first 20 minutes, a retrace to its opening price is
  high probability.

Key line: *"I've eliminated 50% of the guesswork. I can only short it. I don't care if the market
trades 500 points to the upside from here, I cannot buy it."*

### Layer 3 — STRUCTURE: dealing ranges and liquidity

- A **dealing range** is the space between *the most recent high that pushed the market lower* and
  *the most recent low that pushed the market higher*. Not any high/low — the most recent
  *causative* ones. He repeats this definition more than any other idea in 16 videos, which tells
  you it's the concept students fail on.
- **Buy-side liquidity** rests above highs, **sell-side** below lows. Price doesn't move randomly;
  it moves to collect liquidity and pair orders.
- **Failure swings** — a low that failed to trade below the prior low. These stack liquidity and are
  low-resistance, so they're the easiest targets. His preferred take-profit areas.
- **Never trade at the midpoint.** Buy at a discount, sell at a premium, act only at range extremes.
- **Trend read:** in a bearish market, *up-close candles support price lower*; in a bullish market,
  *down-close candles support price higher*. When that stops working, delivery is shifting.
- **A+ setup = 3+ confluences**: TBR sweep + dealing-range extreme sweep + hourly swing point.
  *"If you have three or more confluences that come together, it's going to be high probability"* —
  and on those he deliberately **risks more**.

### Layer 4 — TRIGGER: the three entry models

Taught as a ranked set, which is good webinar structure as-is:

1. **Inverse Fair Value Gap — the earliest entry.** A bullish gap that price fails to hold and
   closes below (or vice versa). *"Price tried to go one way and couldn't. The failure is the
   signal."*
2. **Order Block made in liquidity — the strongest entry.** Definition he gives: *"an order block is
   defined by a change in the state of delivery"* — the last opposing candle before a strong move.
   Crucially, **only the one that swept the TBR high/low counts**. *"Not just any old OB."*
3. **Three-candle rotation — the backup entry.** Price closes above/below three consecutive opposing
   candles. Use when neither of the above is present.

Bonus model (given only to viewers who stay to the end): **project off the TBR bodies** from −2.5 to
−5 to estimate how far expansion runs.

### Reversal sequences — confirmation the sweep will actually reverse
1. **Double SMT / double divergence** (highest conviction) — NQ and ES diverge *twice at the same
   level*. Shows institutional absorption.
2. **Three-drive reversal** — three consecutive drives into the same pool = exhaustion.
3. **Three-candle rotation** — the execution trigger.

*"If you see all three of these together, just know this market is definitely going to reverse."*

### Layer 5 — EXIT: grading the price swing

The piece he says matters more than entries — *"I would argue exits are more important than
entries"* — and the one most likely to be underweighted in a webinar.

Fibonacci from the relevant swing high to swing low, with levels **0 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1**:

| Zone | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0 → 0.25 | Delivery beginning to shift | Close beyond 0.25 = entry confirmation |
| 0.25 → 0.5 | Momentum building | Hold |
| beyond 0.5 | Reversal confirmed; should not return to 0.25 | Move to break-even |
| 0.5 → 0.75 | Prime profit zone | Scale out / take profit |
| 0.75 → 1 | Extended | Full TP. **Never open a new position here.** |

Plus the fixed alternative: **50 points on NQ and you're done.** *"50 points every single day is
going to compound... You won't go broke taking profit. You will go broke taking losses."*
Wants to be flat and off the charts by 9:42–10:00.

## 8. My read: how this maps onto the webinar (recommendations)

### The paradigm / shift
Rauf already has the strongest possible candidate and states it flatly:

> **"Patterns don't repeat. Time does."**

That is a genuine "oh, this changes everything" — it reframes every failed strategy the prospect has
ever bought as a *timing* failure rather than a *them* failure. It passes Fladlien's test cleanly:
*what I thought was my problem (I can't read the chart well enough) is not the problem at all.*

Two Fladlien moves are already latent in his material and should be made explicit:

- **Change the circumstances, not the person.** He isn't asking them to become better analysts. He's
  asking them to show up at one specific hour and do less. That is the "find the table of bad poker
  players" move, and it's why the offer is believable to someone who has failed repeatedly.
- **Smaller results sooner beat larger results later.** Do not lead with $190,000. Lead with
  **50 points, done by 9:42am.** Everyone can picture 50 points; nobody can picture $190k. And it
  deletes the "I'll quit before it works" excuse, because the result arrives tomorrow morning.

The limitation-to-advantage flip writes itself: **having only 30 minutes a day is the edge, not the
handicap.** He says it himself — *"I hate sitting in front of the markets all day."*

Mirrors his own teaching order, each one seeding a bonus:

## 9. Compliance — flag this before we write a word

Several claims that work on YouTube will be a problem on a paid-traffic webinar with a landing page
and a call-booking funnel:

- *"Mathematically unbeatable"* (a video title)
- *"Almost guaranteeing you to be profitable every single month"*
- *"That yields us 100% win rate"*
- *"Statistically proven"* attached to unspecified samples

Trading education on Meta needs an earnings disclaimer, no guaranteed-profit language, and results
framed as past performance with risk disclosure — Heik's course flags exactly this: a non-compliant
landing page gets the ad account rejected. The data is strong enough to survive careful phrasing
("in this sample, over these dates, X% of days"), so we lose nothing real by tightening it. I'd
rather raise this now than rewrite the deck later.
