## 3.3 Strategy — the three triggers

[SLIDE: three triggers, ranked]
1. **Inverse fair value gap** — earliest
2. **Order block made in liquidity** — strongest
3. **Three candle rotation** — backup

I use three. And I want to show you the trade-off between them up front, because that's the bit that
actually matters.

They're ranked earliest to latest. Which means they're also ranked *best price* to *most certain*.

The earlier you go, the better your entry and the more you can be wrong. The later you go, the worse
your price and the more sure you are. There's no perfect one. There's the right one for the day.

### Trigger 1 — the inverse fair value gap

A fair value gap is just a hole. Price moved so fast it skipped a level and left a gap behind it.

Normally when price comes back to that hole, it bounces off it and carries on the way it was going.
That's what it's supposed to do.

An inverse fair value gap is when it comes back — and goes straight through it.

The gap failed. And that failure is the signal. Price tried to go one way and couldn't.

[LIVE CHART] *(Ad-lib:)*
- Show the gap forming on the push down.
- "This should hold. If we're still going lower, this holds."
- Price closes above it.
- "It didn't hold. It failed. That's my earliest sign the whole thing's turning."
- Mark the entry.

### Trigger 2 — the order block made in liquidity

This is the strongest one and it's the one I take most.

An order block is the last opposite-coloured candle before a big move. Before a big move up, it's the
last red candle. Everybody teaches that much.

Here's the part they don't teach, and it's the entire difference between this working and not working:

[SLIDE: "Not just any order block."]

It only counts if it's *the candle that swept the level.*

Not any red candle. The specific one that poked below the low of my range and took the stops out.
That's the candle that did the damage. That's where the orders were.

Any other red candle on that chart is just a red candle.

[LIVE CHART] *(Ad-lib:)*
- Show three or four red candles in the leg down.
- "Which one? Not that one. Not that one. This one — because this is the one that went below the low."
- Close above it.
- "Now when price comes back into it, it should push away hard. That's my entry."

### Trigger 3 — the three candle rotation

And this one's the simplest thing in the world.

Price is dropping. Count three red candles in a row. When a candle closes above all three of them,
the market has rotated. That's your signal.

Reverse it for shorts — three green, close below all three.

[LIVE CHART] *(Ad-lib:)*
- Count them out loud. One, two, three.
- Show the close above.
- "That's it. That's the whole thing."

I use this when the other two aren't there. It's the latest signal so it's the worst price — but it's
the most certain, and some days that's the trade-off you want.

## 3.5 The confirmation layer

Now — one more layer, and then we'll do exits.

Sometimes price sweeps the level and just... keeps going. And you're sat there waiting for a trigger
that isn't coming.

Three things I look for that tell me the turn is actually real:

**Double divergence.** Nasdaq and S&P are supposed to move together. When one makes a new low and
the other refuses to — twice, at the same level — somebody's absorbing that selling. That's my
highest conviction signal on the chart.

**Three drives.** Price drives into the same area three times. Third one's exhaustion. It doesn't
need to go back a fourth time.

**The rotation** — which you've already seen.

[LIVE CHART] *(Ad-lib:)*
- Show a day with drives one, two, three into the low.
- Overlay ES. Show the divergence.
- "So we've got three drives, we've got the divergence, and now we've got the rotation."
- "That's when I'm in."

And this is my rule for everything, write this one down — **three things pointing the same way and I
take the trade. Three things pointing against me and I don't.** That's how you stop the guessing.

## 3.6 Exits

[SLIDE: "Exits matter more than entries."]

Right. Now this is the part I care about most and I'd argue it's more important than everything I've
