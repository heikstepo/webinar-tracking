# Inside each step — steps-within-the-step vs criteria

Fladlien, *One to Many*, ch. 7 (STRATEGY AND COMMITMENT / CRITERIA).

> "There are two ways I rely on teaching the 'how' of the content portion of a webinar:
> steps or criteria… But it's always 'steps within steps' or criteria."

**Steps within the step** = a sequence. *Do this, then this, then this.*
His Amazon step one (product selection) had four:
1. Pick products with proven buyer demand
2. Demand starts with best-seller rank in every top-level category
3. Filter that demand to an income stream you'd like in your life
4. Use tools to assist

**Criteria** = *"If you want to do X, here is what you must consider."*
His Amazon step two (sourcing) had five factors: low initial order quantity · lead time ·
profit potential · product quality · sourcing options.

**How he chooses:** by gut. But he gives a real diagnostic —
> "I used to do step three step-by-step. It worked out okay. Then I switched to a criteria
> approach and it just flowed so much better. I could tell because of the commitment I got
> after teaching step three."

**So: measure the pop on the commitment after each step. Weak pop = wrong approach. Switch it.**

# Our four steps

| Step | Approach | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Window | **Steps** | It's a procedure they run tomorrow morning. Concrete, chronological, four clean sub-steps. |
| 2. The Bias | **Criteria** | As a sequence it's only two moves — too thin. As criteria the *no-trade day* falls out naturally, which is the credibility beat. |
| 3. The Trigger | **Criteria** | It's a menu with a trade-off, not an order of operations. "Which one is yours" is a better question than "what's next." |
| 4. The Exit | **Steps** | The graded price swing IS chronological — it's the levels price walks through while you hold. |

Note the alternation: steps → criteria → criteria → steps. That solves monotony on its own.
Four identically-shaped blocks is the fastest way to lose a room.

---

## STEP 1 — THE WINDOW · steps within the step

1. **Know the two windows.** London 1:12–2:12. New York 8:12–9:12. Eastern.
2. **Mark the high and the low of that hour.** Two lines. That's the range.
3. **Wait for one side to get taken.** Buy side above, sell side below.
4. **Target the opposing end.** Sweep one side, trade to the other.

Then the three rules land as the boundary conditions on the procedure:
don't trade during the window · trade 9:30–10:00, two max · nothing by ten, no trade.

**Time: 30–35% of the content section.** Fladlien's reason, verbatim in effect —
lose them on step one and you lose them for good, and pacing should start slow
and hit a frenzy as the pitch starts.

**Commitment (as a choice, not a yes/no):**
> "Which window are you trading — London or New York? Type it in the chat."

**Transition (as if already done):**
> "Now that you've got your range marked…"

---

## STEP 2 — THE BIAS · criteria

Frame: *"Before I'll tell you I'm a buyer, four things have to be true."*

1. **The hourly candle broke one side.** 8am for New York, 1am for London. Breaks the low → bullish. Breaks the high → bearish.
2. **It broke one — not neither.** Indecisive candle = no trade. Hop off the charts.
3. **The range formed inside a dealing range.** His words: *"we want to see our time-based range actually form within this."*
4. **You're not at equilibrium.** Never trade the midpoint.

Why criteria beats steps here: as a sequence this is "look at candle, read direction" —
two moves, and Fladlien's rule is that too few sub-steps means you merged something.
As criteria, each condition is a filter, and **the days you don't trade come out of the
framework instead of being an excuse bolted on afterwards.** That's the difference between
"sometimes I sit out" sounding like a system and sounding like a hedge.

The liquidity logic (break the low → buy) is not a criterion. It's the **vision** beat that
runs before them — it's the belief flip, and it needs its own room.

**Commitment:**
> "Which of those four would you have got wrong yesterday? Type the number."

**Transition:**
> "Now that you know which way you're going…"

---

## STEP 3 — THE TRIGGER · criteria

Frame: *"Three ways in. Ranked earliest to latest — which is the same as best price to most certain. There's no perfect one, there's the right one for the day."*

1. **Inverse fair value gap** — earliest. Best price, least confirmation.
2. **Order block made in liquidity** — the strongest, and the one he takes most.
3. **Three candle rotation** — latest. Worst price, most certain.

The criterion isn't which is correct, it's **where you sit on the price-vs-certainty
trade-off.** That's a genuine "here is what you must consider" frame, and it turns a
technical menu into a decision about the attendee.

**Fladlien's flash technique applies here.** He advances the category slide faster than
they can write it down — deliberately, to force attention and instil a gentle fear of loss.
Flash the three, then go back and teach each one.

**Commitment — the strongest one in the webinar, and it's Fladlien's exact form:**
> "Which of the three is going to be *your* trigger? Type 1, 2 or 3."

His version: *"So which of these options are you going to first use when you go out and find
the manufacturer…"* — the question presupposes they're doing it.

**Transition:**
> "Now that you're in the trade…"

---

## STEP 4 — THE EXIT · steps within the step

The graded price swing, as the four things that happen while you're holding:

1. **Close past 25** — the market's shifting. That's the confirmation.
2. **Close past 50** — it shouldn't come back to 25. Move to break even.
3. **50 to 75** — the profit zone. Take money off.
4. **Past 75** — extended. Full take profit. Never a new position up there.

Then the point-and-click version underneath it: *fifty points on the Nasdaq and you're done.*

This is chronological by nature — it's the trade breathing. Forcing it into criteria
would break the one thing that makes it teachable.

**Commitment:**
> "Are you taking the graded swing or the flat fifty? Type A or B."

# The three mechanics we're currently missing

## 1. Continuity between steps — the hula girl rule

> "Notice the hula girl and the knife sheath. Those were also products that I showed in step
> one as they related to profit potential. **The better you have continuity between steps,
> the more powerful each step becomes.**"

Right now every step uses different charts. It shouldn't.

**Pick one anchor day and thread it through all four steps.** Same date, same chart,
revisited four times, deeper each time:
- Step 1 — here's the box on it
- Step 2 — here's the 8am candle on the same day
- Step 3 — here's where I actually got in on that day
- Step 4 — and here's how I got out of it

Four steps stop feeling like four topics and start feeling like one trade you've been
building the whole time. The repeat-stacks (five similar days side by side) still run
alongside — they do a different job, which is proving repeatability.

**Then the bridge uses a brand new day**, so they see it generalise off the anchor.

## 2. Persuasional foreplay — per step

Show how critical something is and how slow/painful it is manually. Don't say you'll give
it to them. Fladlien's own correction on this is worth quoting because it reversed his
earlier belief:

> "Trying to meticulously give them a free, manual, labor-intensive version they can use
> independent of buying or not, and then positioning my turnkey option at the end as vastly
> superior… not only cost me a lot of sales, but it didn't seem to actually help my audience."

Where the gap is in each of our steps:
- **Step 1** — the other ranges beyond London and NY *(⚠️ unverified — does he actually teach more? needs confirming before it's seeded)*
- **Step 2** — nobody checking your read. You can learn the four criteria today and still misapply them for six months alone.
- **Step 3** — "am I seeing a real order block or am I seeing what I want to see?" This is the highest-demand gap in the whole webinar and it's pure feedback.
- **Step 4** — the payout mechanics. This is the direct bridge to the offer, so it carries the most weight.

**⚠️ One thing NOT to seed: an indicator or auto-marking template.** He's explicitly
anti-indicator on the channel — *"indicators, random predictions that you make and hope
within the market."* Seeding a tool bonus would contradict his own positioning.

## 3. Commitments phrased as choices

Every step above closes with a *which one*, not a *yes/no*. That's deliberate — a yes/no
can be answered without imagining yourself doing it. "Type 1, 2 or 3" cannot.

And every transition is phrased as if the step is already done. Fladlien:
> "Note that I phrased it as if they have already done it. This creates ownership, which
> makes it easier to get them to buy the product you sell at the end."
