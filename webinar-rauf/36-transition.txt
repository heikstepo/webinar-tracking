STRUCTURE (playbook section 5)

1. Recap — 12 points, all phrased as things they now HAVE, not things Rauf covered.
   Ends on "an hour ago almost none of you had any of that", which is the value receipt.
2. Yes-ladder — 4 rungs. The last one ("would you want to go further") is the bridge:
   the only way to go further is to buy.
3. The 20% / NDA reveal — sits between the ladder and the two choices deliberately.
   The ladder asks "would you go further"; this is what proves a further exists.
4. Two choices, with Heik's upgrade: the specific failures between the options.
5. Permission to pitch. They type 2, then they type yes.

WHY THE GO-ALONE LIST IS BUILT THE WAY IT IS

Every failure on that list is a strategy-execution failure, not a discipline failure.
That's deliberate — the intro's whole argument is "psychology problems are strategy
problems". If the go-alone list said "you'll lack discipline", it would contradict the
intro and hand back the excuse we spent an hour taking away.

It also maps 1:1 onto the offer:
 - "two of those weren't my setup"          -> personal trade reviews
 - "you got away with it and learned wrong" -> personal trade reviews + 1on1 calls
 - "you changed something on a Tuesday"     -> accountability check-ins, DM support
 - "failed on a rule you didn't know"       -> prop firm playbooks
 - "you'll pay to sit it again"             -> Rauf pays for the first challenge

So by the time the offer appears, every component is already the answer to a problem
they just agreed they'd have.

⚠️ THREE THINGS I NEED RAUF TO CONFIRM

- "About 20% of what I actually run" — is 20% the honest number? Any number here is a
  factual claim about his business. If he's not sure, say "a fraction of" instead.
- The NDA. I have not found this anywhere in his public material. If students really do
  sign one, it's a strong line. If it's approximate, cut it — it's the kind of detail
  people ask about on the call.
- Reason 1 (giving out more ranges degrades them). This is a logical argument, not
  something I've heard him say. It's the standard edge-decay argument and it holds up,
  but he should be willing to defend it live because someone in the Q&A will push on it.

Reason 2 needs no sign-off — it's just true, and it's the one that actually sells the
1-on-1.

WHAT I DELIBERATELY DIDN'T DO

- No price. High-ticket book-a-call model: the CTA is the application, the closer handles
  price. Bonus values still get anchored in the pitch.
- No "I'm going to make you an offer now" apology. He already got permission in the intro
  ("Then I'll make you an offer at the end to help you implement it. Sound good?"), and
  he gets it again here. Twice is enough; a third apology reads as insecurity.
