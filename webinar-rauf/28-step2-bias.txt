So we've got our 2 boxes, and they tell us where the orders are and when the market's
going to go for them.


What they don't tell us yet is which way we're trading.


And this is the part everyone assumes is the hard part, because everybody will tell you
that you can't predict the market.


That's rubbish. Predicting is exactly what we're doing here — it's the whole point.


The difference is what you're predicting from.


Most traders predict from an opinion. They check the news, glance at a higher timeframe,
decide the market feels bearish, and hope they're right.


That's not a prediction. That's a guess with a nicer word on it.


And here's what that actually looks like on a normal day.


* Market spikes
* You buy
* It reverses
* Stops you out
* You're annoyed, so you flip and sell
* And it reverses again


Anyone done that? Put it in the chat.


And that's not a discipline problem. You're making the biggest decision of your entire
day in the most violent 30 seconds of the session, with nothing in front of you to base
it on.


Nobody makes a good decision in that state… nobody.


So here's what we do instead, and this is the part that took the pressure off me
completely.


We don't predict before the market has shown us anything. We wait for it to do one
specific thing first, and then we anticipate what comes next.


Because before the market can make a real move in either direction, it has to go and
collect the orders sat on one side.


And you already know exactly where those orders are, because you marked them in step 1.


So the market is about to walk into one of your boxes, and the second it does, you can
anticipate where it's going next.


That's the difference between a reactive trader and an anticipative one.


So by the end of this section you'll sit down at 9:29 tomorrow, watch one thing happen,
and know whether you're a buyer or a seller today, with an actual reason behind it.


Sounds good?
