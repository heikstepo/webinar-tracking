STEP 1 — THE TIME BASED RANGES
what you mark before the open
=========================


-- context --


Now, as we already said — time within the market is by far the most important part of my strategy.


Most inconsistent traders I talk to aren't bad at trading. Their timing is just wrong.


And that's actually a good thing.


Because if you fix that one problem and only trade at certain times of day…


You won't have to sit there for hours on end waiting for your patterns to form.


You get in at the same time every single day.


Analyse the charts, wait for your bias.


And if you see something in that window, you trade, and you're out fast.


If not, you don't trade, and you're also out fast.


That simple. It adds a lot of clarity to your trading and it takes out the stress.


Because you don't have to do too much thinking by yourself —


the charts within that 1 hour window tell you exactly what to do.


So let's get into what those time windows are and how to mark them.




-- before I give you the times --


Now, I've got a number of time based ranges.


Today I'm going to leak 2 of them, since you're live with me here.


One for the New York session, and one for London.


⚠️ [NDA line goes here if Rauf confirms it — see note at the bottom of this file]


But back to the 2 you're getting today.


All of them sit inside specific dealing ranges.


So before I show you what the ranges are and how to mark them,


I have to first show you how I identify those dealing ranges.




-- the dealing range --


A dealing range is the space between a significant swing high and a swing low.


And a swing high and a swing low just means a point where the market turned around.


SHOW LIVE
[Chart with a clear swing high and swing low. Mark both.]

LIVE START


So — this was the low that pushed the market higher.


And this was the high that pushed the market lower.


Draw a line at each.


Automatically, that becomes a range to the market.


LIVE END


That's it. Super easy.


Now why does that matter?


Because of what sits above and below those 2 lines.


SHOW LIVE
[Same chart. Stack order markers above the high, then below the low, as he says each one.]

LIVE START


An order is just you telling the market "if price gets here, I'm in."


You set it, you walk away, and it fires by itself when price arrives.


Now — everyone looking at this chart can see that high and that low. Same as you can.


So they all leave their orders in roughly the same place.


Which means there's a stack of them sat right there, waiting to get filled.


LIVE END


That's all liquidity means. Orders waiting to get filled.


And the market doesn't move to random places. It moves to where the orders are.


So the moment you draw those 2 lines, you know where the money is parked.


But honestly — it doesn't tell you when anything happens. That box could be days wide.


So the next thing we mark is our time based ranges.




-- the time based range --


This is the inner box. And this one is about time, not price.


Here are the times.


My London range starts at 1:12 and finishes at 2:12.


New York runs 8:12 to 9:12. All Eastern Standard Time.


I'll work in New York today, because that's the one most of you can be awake for.


SHOW LIVE
[Same chart. Drop the 8:12-9:12 box inside the dealing range. Mark its high and low.]

LIVE START


8:12 it starts. 9:12 it's done.


High there. Low there.


2 more lines. That's your time based range.


LIVE END


And now here's the rule that connects the 2 boxes.


I want to see my time based range form inside the dealing range.


If it hasn't, I'm not interested.


Because if my hour formed somewhere price has already been and moved past,


then there's nothing left sitting there for the market to go and collect.


The dealing range is what tells you your hour landed somewhere that still matters.


Makes sense? Put a yes in the chat.




-- does the market actually do anything at these lines --


Now, you've just watched me draw 2 boxes on a chart.


So the fair question is — does the market actually do anything at those lines? Or are they just lines?


Don't take my word for it. Data never lies. People can lie.


SHOW LIVE
[Edgeful. Their branding visible. 128 sessions, January to July.]

LIVE START


128 sessions. Not my data. Theirs.


Zero.


Zero days out of 128 where the market didn't take one of those lines.


Not usually. Not most of the time. There wasn't a single day it ignored the box.


LIVE END


Now — someone smart will spot the catch, so I'll say it first.


That's measured across the whole session, and I only trade one window of it.


Some days it happened at 2pm and I'd long gone. That's why some days I don't trade.


But the level gets respected every single day. You are never sat there hoping something shows up.




-- handoff --


So now you've got a box inside a box.


The big one tells you where the orders are.


The small one tells you when the market goes for them.


But what neither of them tells you is: where's the market going?


That's step 2. The bias.
